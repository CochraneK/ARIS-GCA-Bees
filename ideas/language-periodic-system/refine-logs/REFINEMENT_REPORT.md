# Refinement Report · Language Periodic System → Predictive Geometry

## Initial idea

Construct or discover a “periodic table of human language” in which recurrent structural primitives organize cross-linguistic diversity.

## What the ARIS pass changed

### 1. Novelty was narrowed

The original metaphor was not novel. Baker (2001) explicitly proposed a periodic table of languages, and later topology-of-syntax work already studied non-trivial geometry and persistent loops in syntactic-parameter spaces.

The surviving contribution became:

> turn the historical periodic-table analogy into a falsifiable predictive model class and compare it out of sample against non-periodic alternatives on modern dependency-curated data.

### 2. The research target changed from visualization to model competition

A useful periodic table cannot be justified because a 2-D plot looks circular. The candidate therefore moved to held-out prediction of cross-linguistic feature associations, including family-held-out evaluation.

### 3. The data rejected the simplest global periodic framing

Stage 1 showed that a simple circle carried reproducible information but underperformed tree/graph alternatives under family hold-out.

### 4. A stronger periodic model was tested instead of accepting a strawman rejection

Stage 1B fixed spectral connectivity and directly optimized angular feature positions. The result became mixed: periodic prediction could roughly match tree at 40 features but lost again at 60 features.

### 5. Local periodicity was tested without cherry-picking

Stage 1C used TLI's own predefined feature groupings and required four valid family-held-out replicates per domain. No domain satisfied the predeclared joint threshold for predictive competitiveness and circular-order stability.

### 6. The manuscript framing was therefore revised

Current working title:

> **Beyond the Periodic Table: Predictive Geometry of Cross-Linguistic Structural Space**

The periodic-table hypothesis remains the historical motivation and a model to be fairly tested, but it is no longer the assumed answer.

## What was deliberately not done

- no decorative periodic-table figure was elevated to evidence;
- no claim of linguistic “atoms” as natural kinds;
- no novelty claim based on persistent homology alone;
- no post-hoc selection of the 40-feature subset as the final result;
- no fake ARIS reviewer receipt;
- no paper number consumed before the research identity stabilizes.

## Current scientific interpretation

Cross-linguistic feature relations show reproducible structure beyond a shuffle null. Some circular orderings are stable, especially for grammar linear order, but stability of an ordering does not imply that circular geometry is the best predictive model. The current evidence is more consistent with **structured, heterogeneous, predominantly non-periodic geometry** than with a single periodic system.

This remains an exploratory conclusion until a standards-aligned circular-seriation check, spatial validation, stronger uncertainty/capacity analysis, and external replication are complete.

## Remaining blockers

1. standards-aligned circular-seriation / circular-Robinson sensitivity;
2. geography-aware validation;
3. paired uncertainty and model-capacity analysis;
4. second dataset / independent replication;
5. identity-bearing ARIS secondary review.

## Refined recommendation

Continue the candidate, but continue it as a **predictive geometry study** rather than a periodic-table construction project. The strongest outcome may be a rigorous empirical boundary on the periodic-table metaphor rather than a periodic table itself.
