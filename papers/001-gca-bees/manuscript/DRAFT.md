# Uncertainty Monitoring and Cross-Task Cognitive Covariation in Honey Bees

## Stage-1 research-design manuscript

### Abstract

Honey bees show two relevant but currently disconnected empirical patterns. First, individuals can display correlated performance across multiple learning tasks. Second, bees can adaptively avoid difficult decisions in an opt-out paradigm, a behavior interpreted either as uncertainty monitoring or as the product of learned associations. Earlier versions of this project imposed a single latent precision parameter in simulation and then interpreted the resulting covariance as evidence for a unified mechanism. We replace that circular strategy with a falsifiable model-comparison framework. The confirmatory analysis will compare a one-factor model, correlated and independent two-factor models, trial-local associative models, and a hybrid model. A precision-like account is retained only as exploratory and must outperform simpler alternatives on held-out empirical data. Existing publications are sufficient to motivate the models but not to estimate the coupling between learning ability and uncertainty-sensitive choice because the relevant phenotypes have not been measured together in the same individuals. The present paper therefore defines an evidence audit, data-acquisition gate, model-specification lock, synthetic model-recovery diagnostic, and a same-individual experimental design. The main scientific outcome is intentionally sign-neutral: evidence for a common factor, separable factors, an associative account, or no stable cross-domain coupling would each update theories of insect cognition.

## Introduction

The honey bee is a useful system for studying individual cognitive variation because experimentally tractable learning paradigms can be repeated within individuals. Finke et al. (2023) reported positive consistency across simple discrimination, reversal learning and negative patterning, although not all pairwise relationships were statistically reliable. A later factor-analytic reanalysis proposed GCA-like factors for visual and olfactory batteries.

A separate literature addresses difficulty-sensitive decision avoidance. Perry and Barron (2013) found that honey bees could use an opt-out route more often when discriminations were difficult and sometimes transfer the opt-out policy to a novel situation. Importantly, the authors did not claim that the behavior uniquely demonstrated introspective metacognition: they explicitly considered associative value learning as an alternative. A 2025 review of insect consciousness retains the same uncertainty.

The missing observation is therefore not another simulation. It is the covariance between these phenotypes in the same bees, together with trial-level information capable of discriminating latent uncertainty control from reinforcement-driven choice.

## Hypotheses and models

**H1 / M1:** a single domain-general factor explains both learning and uncertainty/control indicators.

**H2 / M2:** learning and uncertainty/control form two correlated latent factors.

**H3 / M3:** the two domains are separable and their latent correlation is effectively zero.

**H4 / M4:** opt-out behavior is adequately predicted by task difficulty, learned action values and reinforcement history without a latent metacognitive state.

**H5 / M5:** a stable individual cognitive factor affects baseline performance, while opt-out decisions are still generated by associative trial processes.

A predictive-precision account is not confirmatory. It may be fitted after the above models only if the empirical design identifies its parameters.

## Methods

### Evidence audit

All strong claims are linked to a source-specific evidence table. Social learning, tool use, metacognition, self-awareness and consciousness are treated as distinct constructs and are not used interchangeably.

### Empirical design

The preferred confirmatory dataset tracks individually marked bees across a compact battery with at least three learning indicators and three uncertainty/control indicators. Colony, task order, reward sensitivity, latency, trial difficulty and attrition are retained. If a complete battery produces unacceptable attrition, a balanced planned-missingness design will be used, provided the overlap pattern identifies all target covariances.

### Statistical analysis

Measurement models will be compared using held-out predictive performance and posterior predictive checks. Trial-level opt-out models will be hierarchical and include bee and colony effects. Exclusion rules and scoring transformations will be fixed before outcome analysis.

### Synthetic model recovery

Synthetic Gaussian latent-variable simulations are used only to test whether the proposed factor structures are distinguishable under plausible hypothetical reliabilities. They do not estimate bee cognition and are never combined with empirical results.

## Expected contribution

This framework converts a mechanism-first simulation into a data-first falsification problem. The strongest possible conclusion will be conditional on empirical model comparison. If a one-factor model wins, a domain-general account becomes more plausible. If a two-factor or associative model wins, the original single-precision theory is weakened or rejected. Either outcome is informative.
