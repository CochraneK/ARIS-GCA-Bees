# Cover letter · Frontiers in Psychology / Comparative Psychology

Dear Editors of *Frontiers in Psychology*, Comparative Psychology,

Please consider the manuscript **“From General Learning Ability to Uncertainty Monitoring in Honey Bees: A Quantitative Synthesis and Falsifiable Model-Comparison Framework”** for publication as a **Hypothesis and Theory** article.

The manuscript addresses a specific gap in comparative cognition. Honey-bee studies have separately reported structured individual differences across learning tasks and adaptive opt-out behavior under uncertainty. These findings are often discussed using broad labels such as general cognitive ability or metacognition, yet the relevant phenotypes have not been measured together in the same individuals. The manuscript therefore asks a stricter question: what does the existing evidence establish, which mechanisms remain observationally equivalent, and what experiment would discriminate them?

The paper contributes a reproducible synthesis of published learning correlations, a cross-modality comparison of published factor-loading structures, a clear separation of uncertainty-sensitive choice from stronger metacognitive and consciousness claims, and five competing models that can be falsified in a future same-individual design. Its central conclusion is deliberately constrained: statistical generality in learning is not yet evidence for a shared biological mechanism linking learning ability to uncertainty monitoring.

The manuscript should fit the Comparative Psychology section because its primary focus is animal learning, individual cognitive variation, decision making, and testable comparative models. No new animal experiments were conducted for this manuscript. All quantitative inputs are traceable to published sources, and the analysis code and machine-readable outputs are openly available in the ARIS4C repository.
