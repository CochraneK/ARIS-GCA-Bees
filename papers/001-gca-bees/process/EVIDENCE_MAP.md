# EVIDENCE MAP · ARIS4C001

## Core sources

### Perry & Barron 2013
**Honey bees selectively avoid difficult choices.** PNAS 110(47):19155–19159. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1314571110.

What it supports:
- difficulty manipulation changed accuracy;
- bees used an opt-out route more on difficult trials at group level;
- opt-out availability could improve performance;
- 10 bees completed the long difficulty/opt-out stage;
- 4 of those 10 showed transfer of opting out to confusing stimuli in the novel task.

What it does not settle:
- whether behavior requires metacognitive access;
- whether associative value learning suffices;
- whether the phenotype covaries with a general cognitive factor.

The article itself explicitly discusses an associative alternative.

### Finke, Scheiner, Giurfa & Avarguès-Weber 2023
**Individual consistency in the learning abilities of honey bees: cognitive specialization within sensory and reinforcement modalities.** Animal Cognition 26:909–928. DOI: 10.1007/s10071-022-01741-2.

What it supports:
- individual performance can covary across honey-bee learning tasks;
- in the visual free-flying experiment, first-phase reversal correlated with second-phase reversal (rho=0.53) and negative patterning (rho=0.42), whereas second-phase reversal vs negative patterning was not significant (rho=0.25);
- analogous olfactory analyses also showed positive but heterogeneous relations.

What it does not establish:
- a unique one-factor GCA model;
- any relation to opt-out uncertainty behavior.

### Peñaherrera-Aguirre et al. 2024
**Possible evidence for the Law of General Intelligence in honeybees (Apis mellifera).** Intelligence 106:101856. DOI: 10.1016/j.intell.2024.101856.

What it supports:
- a secondary exploratory factor analysis of the Finke et al. correlation matrices;
- reported general factors accounted for 46.8% of visual and 52.3% of olfactory performance variance.

Caution:
- this is a reanalysis/discussion of existing task correlations, not a new same-individual uncertainty-monitoring experiment.

### Chittka, Skeels, Dyakova & Janbon 2025
**The exploration of consciousness in insects.** Philosophical Transactions B 380:20240302. DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2024.0302.

What it supports:
- uncertainty-sensitive bee behavior remains relevant to debates about insect metacognition;
- the review explicitly retains learned-association explanations and calls for further work.

### Bridges et al. 2024
**Bumblebees socially learn behaviour too complex to innovate alone.** Nature 627:572–578. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-07126-4.

Use in this paper:
- background example of complex insect cognition only.

Do not use it as:
- direct evidence of tool use,
- direct evidence of self-awareness,
- direct evidence of a shared precision mechanism.

## Evidence gap

No source above links the Perry-style opt-out phenotype and the Finke-style learning/GCA phenotype in the same individuals. That missing covariance is the central empirical gap of reconstructed Paper 001.
