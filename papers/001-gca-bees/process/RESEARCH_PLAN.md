# RESEARCH PLAN · ARIS4C001

## 1. Research question

When honey bees vary in learning performance and in difficulty-sensitive opt-out behavior, what latent and trial-level model best accounts for that variation?

The paper does not presuppose that the answer is metacognition, self-awareness, predictive coding, GCA, or a single precision variable.

## 2. Primary estimands

### Cross-task structure
Estimate the covariance structure among individual-level indicators of:

- simple discrimination learning,
- reversal learning,
- negative patterning/configural learning,
- opt-out sensitivity to difficulty,
- performance benefit when opt-out is available,
- transfer/generalization of the opt-out policy or an equivalent uncertainty-control indicator.

### Trial-level decision process
Estimate whether opt-out choice is better predicted by:

- objective trial difficulty,
- recent reward/punishment history,
- learned stimulus/action values,
- decision latency,
- subject-level random effects,
- and, only if identified, a latent uncertainty-control state.

## 3. Confirmatory models

### M1: one-factor model
All six indicators load on a single standardized latent factor. This is the strongest domain-general hypothesis.

### M2: two correlated factors
Learning indicators load on G; uncertainty/control indicators load on U. Correlation rho(G,U) is freely estimated.

### M3: two independent factors
Same measurement model as M2 but rho(G,U)=0.

### M4: task-local associative decision model
Opt-out is modeled at trial level from difficulty, reinforcement history and learned action values. No introspective state is required.

### M5: hybrid
A latent individual factor affects baseline learning/decision efficiency while opt-out decisions are still generated by learned trial-level values.

### Exploratory model: predictive precision
A precision-like latent variable may be compared only after M1–M5 are estimable. No fixed sign, optimum, or neural location is preregistered.

## 4. Evidence hierarchy

1. Same-individual raw trial data.
2. Individual summary data with documented task scoring.
3. Published covariance/correlation matrices.
4. Digitized figures only for sensitivity analyses.
5. Group means are descriptive and cannot identify individual latent coupling.
6. Synthetic data are restricted to model recovery and power/design diagnostics.

## 5. Sampling and design

The 2013 opt-out study narrowed from 33 trained bees to 10 bees completing the long Stage 3 protocol, illustrating severe attrition risk. The confirmatory design therefore must treat retention as a first-class outcome.

Preferred design:

- at least 6 colonies,
- individual bee IDs retained across tasks,
- task order randomized or counterbalanced,
- colony modeled hierarchically,
- reward sensitivity/motivation recorded,
- explicit stopping/attrition reasons,
- no post-hoc exclusion based on the outcome variable,
- enough repeated trials to estimate individual slopes rather than binary learner/non-learner labels where feasible.

A model-recovery pilot is used to choose the sample-size range. The current synthetic scenario suggests that N around 120–160 retained individuals is much more informative for distinguishing correlated versus independent two-factor structures than N around 60. This is not a biological power claim; final planning must use task-specific reliability and attrition estimates.

## 6. Analysis

### Measurement layer
Use CFA/SEM or Bayesian hierarchical latent-variable models. Report uncertainty in loadings and latent correlations. Compare M1–M3 by predictive criteria and posterior predictive checks, not merely variance explained.

### Trial layer
Use hierarchical logistic choice models for opt-out and accuracy. Include bee and colony random effects. Candidate associative predictors are prespecified before testing any latent uncertainty term.

### Model comparison
Primary comparison: out-of-sample predictive performance plus calibration. Secondary: information criteria / Bayes factors where assumptions are satisfied.

### Robustness
- visual versus olfactory modality,
- alternative learning-score definitions,
- inclusion/exclusion of low performers,
- order and colony effects,
- attrition weighting or selection models,
- difficulty coding,
- latency as predictor versus outcome,
- non-Gaussian latent indicators.

## 7. Claims policy

Allowed before new empirical data:
- published studies show difficulty-sensitive opt-out behavior;
- published studies show cross-task learning covariation;
- multiple mechanisms remain viable.

Not allowed before new empirical data:
- bees possess self-awareness;
- a single precision parameter explains the behaviors;
- high-GCA bees are worse at metacognition;
- circadian disruption flips the sign of a metacognition–GCA relationship;
- a particular brain region implements the proposed latent factor.

## 8. Success criteria

The project succeeds even if the best model is associative, domain-specific, or null. A negative result that eliminates the legacy single-precision account is scientifically informative and is not treated as pipeline failure.
