# Cover letter · Linguistic Typology

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the manuscript **“Testing the periodic-table hypothesis of human language: predictive evidence does not support a global circular organization”** for publication as a research paper in *Linguistic Typology*.

The manuscript turns a long-standing “periodic table of languages” analogy into a bounded, falsifiable model-comparison question. Rather than constructing a visually periodic classification, it asks whether a directly optimized global circular geometry predicts held-out cross-linguistic structural feature relations better than non-periodic alternatives. The analysis combines family-held-out prediction, predefined domain tests, direct circular-Robinson-style and wrap-around closure diagnostics, geographic robustness checks, and representation checks using TLI/GBI and separately processed WALS data.

The principal result is negative but informative: the tested global circle captures reproducible structure, yet it is not the strongest held-out account. Hierarchical/non-circular benchmarks perform better under family hold-out, and the learned circular ordering lacks robust global closure. The manuscript explicitly does **not** claim that language has been proven to be tree-shaped or that all forms of linguistic periodicity are impossible. Recent evidence for circular organization within specific semantic domains is treated as compatible with the present global negative result.

The element of novelty is the explicit out-of-sample stress test of a global language-periodic-table hypothesis against non-periodic alternatives, together with direct held-out circularity diagnostics and cross-representation robustness checks. Prior work on linguistic topology, typological prediction, latent structure, and circular seriation is discussed and delimits rather than supplies this contribution.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been prepared for double-anonymised review. All analysed data are from public third-party linguistic resources, and anonymised code/results are supplied as supplementary material for review.
