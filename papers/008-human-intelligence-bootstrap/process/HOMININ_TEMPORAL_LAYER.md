# Hominin temporal layer · Pilot 6

## Purpose

The extant-species matrix asks which configurations covary with higher-order cultural outcomes. The hominin layer asks a different question:

> **Did a proposed prerequisite appear before the outcome it is supposed to enable?**

This layer is deliberately separate from extant-species regression because archaeological evidence has different sampling, dating, preservation and attribution problems.

## Coding rule

Each row is a **first secure evidence interval**, not an estimate of the true evolutionary origin.

Therefore:
- a first archaeological appearance provides an upper bound on when a capability was certainly present;
- absence before that date is weak evidence because many materials do not preserve;
- wood, fire, fibres, adhesives, speech and social norms have especially severe preservation bias;
- a temporal contradiction can reject a narrowly defined observable prerequisite more strongly than temporal precedence can prove causation.

## Pilot milestones

The initial layer spans:
- 3.3 Ma Lomekwi stone manufacture;
- 1.76 Ma Acheulean bifacial technology;
- ~1 Ma in-situ fire at Wonderwerk;
- 780 ka cooking at Gesher Benot Ya'aqov;
- 640–380 ka H. erectus shell engraving at Trinil;
- ~500 ka hafted stone-tipped hunting technology at Kathu Pan 1;
- 476 ± 23 ka structural woodworking at Kalambo Falls;
- the 500–330 ka initial African ochre-use phase;
- ~400 ka deliberate fire-making evidence at Barnham;
- 320–295 ka long-distance obsidian procurement and pigment use at Olorgesailie;
- 315 ± 34 ka early H. sapiens at Jebel Irhoud;
- 160–40 ka habitual African ochre-use phase;
- 150–142 ka shell beads at Bizmoune;
- 100 ka Blombos ochre-processing workshop;
- 73 ka Blombos abstract drawing.

## Immediate falsification results

The temporal layer already rejects several **naive observable necessary-condition claims**.

### Anatomical Homo sapiens is not necessary for many high-level components

Secure archaeological proxies for:
- stone manufacture;
- Acheulean hierarchical technology;
- fire use;
- hafted/composite weapons;
- structural woodworking;
- geometric engraving;

all predate the Jebel Irhoud early-H. sapiens reference.

This does **not** mean earlier hominins had modern human cognition. It means that “being anatomically H. sapiens” cannot serve as a necessary causal explanation for those component capacities.

### Symbolic-material proxies are not necessary for complex technology

Habitual ochre use and personal ornaments are younger than:
- composite hafting;
- structural woodworking;
- long-duration fire use.

So a simple sequence “symbolism first → complex technology later” fails as a universal prerequisite model.

### Fire use, toolmaking and engraving are not sufficient

Fire control, stone toolmaking and pattern engraving all appear far earlier than the later expansion of highly diversified, recombinable cultural systems. Their long temporal lead makes them plausible enabling conditions but poor stand-alone sufficient explanations.

### Deliberate fire-making versus structural construction

The 2026 Barnham evidence places the current earliest secure **fire-making** claim at ~400 ka, while Kalambo's structural wood construction is ~476 ka.

Under a narrow observable definition, deliberate fire-making cannot be a necessary prerequisite for structural woodworking. This does not apply to **fire use/control**, which is much older.

## Why this helps 008

The cross-species layer can say “taxa with X often have Y.”
The hominin layer can say “Y existed before our earliest secure evidence for X.”

Those are complementary falsification routes.

The final analysis should classify candidate conditions as:
- temporally impossible prerequisite;
- temporally compatible prerequisite;
- very early but insufficient alone;
- temporally unresolved because preservation is too poor.
