# Neural harmonization strategy · ARIS4C008

## Why brain mass cannot be a global intelligence stat

The comparative dataset spans primates, elephants, cetaceans, birds, cephalopods and insects. These clades differ in cellular scaling rules, neural packing density, regional allocation and even where substantial computation occurs.

A single cross-animal variable such as brain mass, encephalization quotient or total neuron count would collapse biologically non-equivalent architectures.

## Pilot facts that force a multi-axis treatment

### Human vs elephant: whole-brain neurons reverse the intuitive ranking

The African elephant brain contains about **257 billion neurons**, roughly three times the human average of **86.1 billion**. But about **250.7 billion / 97.5%** of elephant neurons are cerebellar. The elephant cerebral cortex contains about **5.6 billion** neurons compared with roughly **16.3 billion** in humans.

Therefore:
- total neuron number is not equivalent to forebrain computational allocation;
- more neurons than humans is not evidence of greater general cognitive/cultural capacity;
- regional allocation must be retained.

### Birds: small brains can hold primate-like forebrain neuron numbers

Olkowicz et al. showed that parrots and songbirds pack neurons at much higher densities than mammalian brains of equal mass and allocate a high fraction to pallial telencephalon.

Examples in the current panel:
- kea: ~1.281B pallial neurons;
- raven: ~1.204B;
- African grey parrot: ~0.850B;
- Goffin's cockatoo: ~0.599B;
- budgerigar: ~0.149B;
- rock pigeon: ~0.0719B.

Brain mass therefore cannot be compared across birds and mammals as if a gram implied the same computational substrate.

### Cetaceans: measurement method can dominate the estimate

For the northern minke whale, an earlier stereological estimate placed cortical neuron number around **12.8B**. A 2025 isotropic-fractionator study on this species estimated only **3.2B** cortical neurons.

The >4-fold discrepancy is large enough to reverse comparative narratives.

Rule: cetacean neuron estimates from stereology and isotropic fractionator are never pooled without a method term or sensitivity analysis.

### Octopus: much of the nervous system is outside the central brain

Octopus vulgaris is estimated at roughly **500M** neurons in the nervous system, with ~**300M** in arm axial nerve cords and ~**200M** in central/optic structures.

A vertebrate-style cortical-neuron metric therefore has no straightforward cephalopod equivalent.

### Honeybee: sophisticated behavior under an extreme neural budget

Honeybees have only about **1M brain neurons**, yet exhibit social navigation, communication and nontrivial learning.

This makes raw neuron number a poor candidate for a universal necessary condition for many component cognitive abilities.

## Data model

Every neural observation must keep taxon, anatomical structure, neuron count, structure/brain mass where available, counting method, method family, comparability class, exact-species versus calibration/exemplar status, source and uncertainty.

## Confirmatory analysis rules

1. **No global raw-neuron regression.** Do not fit open-endedness against total neurons across the entire animal panel and interpret the coefficient as a general cognitive law.
2. **Use within-comparability analyses.** Preferred contrasts are mammal cerebral-cortex neurons measured with isotropic fractionator, avian pallial neurons measured with isotropic fractionator, within-clade brain-size/neuron residuals, and method-matched sensitivity analyses.
3. **Architecture enters separately from quantity.** Create distinct features for forebrain allocation, neuron packing density, centralization versus distributed processing, cerebellar/sensorimotor allocation, and brain/metabolic cost where comparable.
4. **Cross-clade models use latent/ordinal evidence rather than raw units.** The global neural condition should use uncertainty-aware evidence categories or a hierarchical latent variable with clade and method effects.

## Falsification implications

The current evidence already rejects or weakens several naive hypotheses:

- total neurons above the human level is sufficient → falsified by the elephant contrast;
- large absolute brain mass is sufficient → unsupported by elephants/cetaceans and confounded by cellular scaling;
- a large centralized brain is necessary for sophisticated learning/social transmission → contradicted at component level by insects and cephalopods;
- brain mass alone is a valid universal computational proxy → contradicted by avian neuron packing and cetacean method/scaling results.

None of these observations by itself establishes the sufficient architecture for open-ended human culture.
