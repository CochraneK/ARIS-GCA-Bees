# Closest prior work · ARIS4C008

**Updated:** 2026-09-18

This file defines the novelty boundary. ARIS4C008 must not claim novelty for ideas already present in the literature.

## 1. Laland & Seed (2021) — the closest conceptual predecessor

**Laland, K. N. & Seed, A. M. (2021). Understanding Human Cognitive Uniqueness. Annual Review of Psychology 72:689–716.**  
DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-062220-051256

### What it already establishes
- reviews multiple candidate domains including tool use, problem solving, social cognition and communication;
- argues that no isolated human-only trait adequately explains human cognitive superiority;
- explicitly proposes interactions and mutually reinforcing feedbacks between cognitive domains across developmental, cultural and gene–culture-evolutionary timescales;
- describes humans as cognitive all-rounders rather than the unique possessor of one magic faculty.

### What ARIS4C008 therefore cannot claim
- “first proposal that human uniqueness is an interaction among abilities”;
- “first rejection of a single magic trait”;
- “first network/feedback account of human cognitive uniqueness.”

### Remaining opening
The review is primarily conceptual/synthetic. ARIS4C008 asks whether competing **quantitative architectures**—additive, weakest-link, threshold and interaction/feedback models—can be discriminated using a cross-taxon evidence matrix with phylogenetic, missingness and research-effort controls.

## 2. Mesoudi & Thornton (2018) — necessary/sufficient *criteria* for CCE

**Mesoudi, A. & Thornton, A. (2018). What is cumulative cultural evolution? Proceedings of the Royal Society B 285:20180712.**  
DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2018.0712

### What it already establishes
- distinguishes multiple definitions of cumulative cultural evolution;
- identifies core criteria described as necessary and sufficient for classifying CCE;
- distinguishes extended criteria associated with human CCE;
- argues for finer-grained claims rather than one undifferentiated human/nonhuman Rubicon.

### Novelty boundary
Their “necessary and sufficient” language concerns **criteria defining the phenomenon**, not a macro-comparative inference that a biological/social trait X is a necessary or sufficient *cause* of open-ended cumulative intelligence.

ARIS4C008 must keep these two senses separate:
- **outcome criteria:** what counts as CCE/open-endedness;
- **causal conditions:** what evolutionary configuration permits the outcome.

---

## 3. Dean et al. (2012) — package of sociocognitive processes

**Dean, L. G. et al. (2012). Identification of the social and cognitive processes underlying human cumulative culture. Science 335:1114–1118.**  
DOI: 10.1126/science.1213969

### What it already establishes
Using a staged puzzle task in children, chimpanzees and capuchins, higher-level performance in children was associated with a package including teaching/verbal instruction, imitation and prosociality.

### Novelty boundary
ARIS4C008 cannot claim to be the first study to experimentally associate a **package** of abilities with human cumulative cultural performance.

### Remaining opening
Dean et al. compared three groups/species in one task. ARIS4C008 targets repeated natural evolutionary dissociations across many lineages, tests whether any package generalizes beyond that task, and explicitly searches for taxa possessing the proposed components without the target outcome.

---

## 4. Reader & Laland (2002) — phylogeny + research effort + behavioral flexibility

**Reader, S. M. & Laland, K. N. (2002). Social intelligence, innovation, and enhanced brain size in primates. PNAS 99:4436–4441.**  
PMID: 11891325

### What it already establishes
- comparative primate database of innovation, social learning and tool use;
- positive associations with brain measures;
- phylogenetic correction;
- explicit correction for species research effort.

### Novelty boundary
Neither phylogenetic correction nor research-effort correction is new to ARIS4C008.

### Remaining opening
The outcome was behavioral flexibility/social-learning incidence rather than open-ended cumulative intelligence, and the analysis did not compare explicit additive versus bottleneck/threshold causal architectures.

---

## 5. Reader, Hager & Laland (2011) — primate general/cultural intelligence

**Reader, S. M., Hager, Y. & Laland, K. N. (2011). The evolution of primate general and cultural intelligence. Philosophical Transactions B 366:1017–1027.**  
DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2010.0342

### What it already establishes
- 62 primate species;
- innovation, social learning, tool use, extractive foraging and tactical deception;
- strong covariance across cognitive domains;
- a composite primate general-intelligence/cultural-intelligence factor;
- multiple convergent increases within primates.

### Novelty boundary
The “multidimensional skill-space” intuition alone is not novel, nor is the proposition that cognitive traits covary.

### Remaining opening
ARIS4C008 does not seek a single latent g score. It asks whether **balanced configurations, thresholds or minimum-module bottlenecks** predict higher-order cultural outcomes better than a weighted sum/composite.

## 6. Street et al. (2017) — closest empirical multivariate predecessor

**Street, S. E., Navarrete, A. F., Reader, S. M. & Laland, K. N. (2017). Coevolution of cultural intelligence, extended life history, sociality, and brain size in primates. PNAS 114:7908–7914.**  
DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1620734114

### What it already establishes
Across primates, social-learning proclivity covaries with:
- absolute and relative brain volume;
- reproductive lifespan;
- social group size;
- with phylogenetic comparative analysis and research-effort correction.

The paper explicitly interprets these traits as a coevolving cultural-intelligence cluster.

### Why this is especially close
It already joins several modules central to 008 and includes feedback/co-evolution logic.

### Remaining opening
- primates only;
- social-learning proclivity is the central behavioral outcome;
- no open-ended/cross-domain cumulative-intelligence target;
- no explicit necessary/sufficient-condition falsification;
- no model competition among additive, weakest-link and threshold architectures;
- no deliberate sampling of independent evolutionary dissociations across birds, cetaceans, elephants, cephalopods and insects.

Any manuscript must cite this paper prominently.

## 7. Lefebvre (2013) — birds, primates and fossil hominins

**Lefebvre, L. (2013). Brains, innovations, tools and cultural transmission in birds, non-human primates, and fossil hominins. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 7:245.**  
DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2013.00245

### What it already establishes
Synthesizes convergent links among:
- brain measures;
- innovation;
- tool use;
- cultural transmission;
across birds, nonhuman primates and hominin evidence.

### Remaining opening
It is a comparative review rather than a single cross-taxonomic, source-level, configuration-testing dataset.

## 8. Derex & Mesoudi (2020) — demographic/network structure

**Derex, M. & Mesoudi, A. (2020). Cumulative Cultural Evolution within Evolving Population Structures. Trends in Cognitive Sciences 24:654–667.**  
DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2020.04.005

### What it already establishes
Synthesizes how population size and network structure alter the cultural information pool and discusses feedback between reliance on accumulated knowledge and social networks.

### Novelty boundary
“Network structure matters more than raw group size” is not an original 008 claim.

## 9. Migliano & Vinicius (2022) — network memory / social ratchet

**Migliano, A. B. & Vinicius, L. (2022). The origins of human cumulative culture: from the foraging niche to collective intelligence. Philosophical Transactions B 377:20200317.**  
DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2020.0317

### What it already establishes
Proposes:
- multilevel sociality;
- specialization;
- cultural recombination;
- network memory;
- a social ratchet;
as major ingredients of distinctively powerful human collective intelligence.

### Novelty boundary
ARIS4C008 cannot present network memory or distributed cognition as a newly invented explanation.

### Remaining opening
008 can treat this as one competitor against embodiment, energetic, cognitive and weakest-link alternatives and ask whether similar precursor configurations appear in nonhuman lineages.

## 10. Morgan & Feldman (2025) — open-endedness target

**Morgan, T. J. H. & Feldman, M. W. (2025). Human culture is uniquely open-ended rather than uniquely cumulative. Nature Human Behaviour 9:28–42.**  
DOI: 10.1038/s41562-024-02035-y

### What it already establishes
Argues that:
- cumulative change itself is not uniquely human;
- high transmission fidelity itself is not uniquely human;
- human distinctiveness may be better framed as cultural open-endedness;
- evaluates seven alternative explanatory dimensions.

### Novelty boundary
“Human culture is open-ended rather than merely cumulative” is directly borrowed from this literature and must be attributed.

### Remaining opening
ARIS4C008 operationalizes open-endedness as a graded outcome family and asks which trait configurations best explain transitions toward it.

## 11. Animal Culture Database (Basava et al. 2025)

**Basava, K. et al. (2025). Mapping nonhuman cultures with the Animal Culture Database. Scientific Data 12:1019.**

### What it already establishes
First open comparative relational data infrastructure for documented wild nonhuman cultural behaviours across many taxa.

### Novelty boundary
Building a cross-animal culture database is not itself an 008 contribution.

### Remaining opening
008 integrates culture evidence with life history, energetic/neural proxies, phylogeny, research-effort measures and explicit architecture competition.

# Defensible novelty claim

The strongest defensible contribution is **not** any individual hypothesis.

It is the methodological synthesis:

> **A cross-taxon, phylogenetically explicit, uncertainty-aware test of whether transitions toward open-ended cumulative intelligence are better described by additive trait accumulation, minimum-module bottlenecks, learned thresholds, or interacting feedback configurations—using deliberate evolutionary dissociations to falsify candidate necessary and sufficient causal conditions.**

The contribution has five linked parts:

1. **Outcome decomposition** from social learning through externalization and open-endedness rather than human/nonhuman binary coding.
2. **Configuration inference** rather than one predictor at a time or a single latent intelligence score.
3. **Independent evolutionary dissociations** across primates, birds, cetaceans, elephants, cephalopods and insects.
4. **Bias-aware integration** of research effort, phylogeny, missingness, exact-species provenance and database curation.
5. **Temporal triangulation** with a separate hominin archaeological layer.

# Novelty falsifier

The novelty claim should be withdrawn or substantially narrowed if a prior paper is located that already:

- constructs a comparable multi-clade species-level matrix;
- codes multiple candidate cognitive, social, embodiment, life-history and energetic modules;
- treats cumulative/open-ended culture as an outcome;
- controls phylogeny and research effort;
- explicitly compares additive versus conjunctive/bottleneck/threshold configurations or derives causal necessary/sufficient combinations.

No such paper was identified in the current closest-prior-work search, but this is a **provisional search conclusion**, not proof of absence.
