# Condition ontology · ARIS4C008

The purpose of this ontology is to prevent post-hoc trait shopping. Candidate predictors are grouped before confirmatory analysis.

## A. Generative cognition

Possible indicators:
- innovation rate / innovation breadth;
- reversal learning and behavioural flexibility;
- causal or means-end problem solving;
- planning / delayed action;
- working-memory proxies;
- cross-domain transfer;
- problem-solving diversity rather than one laboratory task.

Do not equate brain size with cognition. Brain size, relative brain size, neuron counts and cognitive performance are separate variables.

## B. Social transmission

Possible indicators:
- experimentally demonstrated social learning;
- social learning of a solution not independently discovered in control conditions;
- imitation / emulation where distinguishable;
- transmission fidelity;
- teaching;
- vertical, horizontal and oblique transmission;
- persistence across cohorts or generations.

A 2024 bumblebee experiment and a 2024 chimpanzee experiment are especially important because both show social acquisition of behaviours that naive individuals failed to innovate alone. Therefore this capacity cannot be treated as uniquely human.

## C. Communication

Possible indicators:
- vocal production learning;
- referential signalling;
- repertoire size;
- combinatorial signalling;
- compositional structure;
- turn-taking / contingent interaction;
- intentional audience-sensitive signalling.

Human language should not be coded as a single binary cause. Its subcomponents should be compared separately.

## D. Manipulation and embodiment

Possible indicators:
- independent effectors capable of object manipulation;
- precision grip / digit or beak-foot coordination / trunk / tentacle control;
- habitual tool manufacture;
- multi-step tool use;
- transport of tools;
- construction behaviour;
- ability to modify objects into persistent functional states.

This module explicitly tests the possibility that cognition needs a physical interface to become cumulative technology.

## E. Externalization / persistent information

Possible indicators:
- durable artifacts;
- modified sites, nests, dams, caches, trails or other persistent structures;
- re-use of prior constructions;
- information recoverable from environmental traces;
- symbolic or representational storage where present.

This is conceptually distinct from manipulation: a species can manipulate well but leave little persistent information.

## F. Social architecture

Possible indicators:
- social tolerance;
- cooperative foraging;
- alloparental care;
- non-kin cooperation;
- fission-fusion dynamics;
- multilevel society;
- cross-group movement;
- network connectivity;
- division of labour / specialization;
- group size, treated separately from network structure.

## G. Life history / learning opportunity

Possible indicators:
- age at maturity;
- juvenile period;
- lifespan;
- generation length;
- parental dependence;
- intergenerational overlap;
- duration of post-reproductive or elder knowledge transfer where relevant.

## H. Energetics

Possible indicators:
- basal or field metabolic rate;
- energy density of diet;
- foraging-time constraints;
- absolute neuron number where available;
- brain metabolic cost;
- parental energetic subsidy;
- food processing / cooking in hominins as a derived external energy-access mechanism.

## I. Ecological challenge and opportunity

Possible indicators:
- extractive foraging;
- diet breadth;
- seasonality;
- environmental variability;
- migration;
- habitat complexity;
- predation risk;
- terrestrial, aquatic, arboreal or aerial constraints on persistent technology.

Ecological complexity is not presumed to monotonically increase intelligence; both challenge and opportunity models will be compared.

## J. Demography and cultural population

Possible indicators:
- effective cultural population size;
- number of accessible models;
- migration / intergroup contact;
- network modularity;
- turnover and replacement;
- population fragmentation.

Census population size alone is inadequate because the theoretically relevant quantity is access to cultural information.

## Candidate causal roles

Each variable can be classified after evidence synthesis as one of:

- **candidate necessary**: outcome rarely/never appears below a threshold;
- **candidate sufficient component**: contributes only in a larger conjunction;
- **enabling**: raises the probability or attainable ceiling but is not required;
- **bottleneck**: the lowest module constrains system performance;
- **amplifier**: strengthens a process once present;
- **consequence / feedback**: partly produced by the outcome itself;
- **proxy**: correlated measurement, not the mechanism;
- **confounder / phylogenetic marker**: must not be interpreted causally.

## Anti-circularity rules

1. Do not define "advanced cognition" using the same cultural behaviours that form the outcome.
2. Do not code human language, human technology or schooling as generic predictors without decomposing them.
3. Do not infer a missing ability from absence of published evidence.
4. Every behavioural code needs an evidence-strength field and research-effort field.
5. Captive experimental ability and naturally expressed wild behaviour must be stored separately.
