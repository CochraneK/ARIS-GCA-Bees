## Why these metrics are not a universal “social complexity score”

Networks differ in:
- interaction definition (grooming, proximity, dominance, contact, etc.);
- captive/free-ranging context;
- observation duration;
- sampling resolution;
- number of observed nodes;
- edge weighting.

Therefore a density of 0.9 in a six-animal captive grooming network is not directly “more socially complex” than density 0.1 in a large free-ranging association network.

ASNR contributes:
- direct network topology;
- interaction-type evidence;
- group/network sample size;
- modularity / transitivity / degree metrics;
- a route to distinguish network structure from simple census group size.

## Role in modules F and J

- **F social architecture:** topology, connectivity, interaction structure.
- **J cultural population:** potentially number of accessible partners, modularity and network reach, but only after interaction/context harmonization.

Raw species medians in the summary are coverage diagnostics, not confirmatory comparative values.
