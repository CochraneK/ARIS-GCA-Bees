# Comparative species panel · ARIS4C008

The panel is designed around contrasts, not a "smartest animals" list.

## Tier 1 · Core high-information contrasts

### Hominids
- Homo sapiens
- Pan troglodytes
- Pan paniscus
- Pongo spp.
- Gorilla spp.

Purpose: closest phylogenetic comparisons, tool traditions, social cognition, tolerance, developmental differences.

### Corvids
- New Caledonian crow
- raven
- selected jays / other corvids with comparable experimental data

Purpose: convergent complex cognition, planning and tool use in a distant neural architecture.

### Parrots
- Goffin's cockatoo
- kea
- African grey parrot
- budgerigar / other vocal-learning parrots where neural and communication data are strong

Purpose: vocal learning + long life + sociality + object manipulation.

### Cetaceans
- bottlenose dolphins
- killer whales
- sperm whales
- humpback whales where culture / song evidence is relevant

Purpose: large brains, socially transmitted repertoires and vocal culture under aquatic embodiment constraints.

### Proboscideans
- African / Asian elephants

Purpose: longevity, social memory, long development, manipulation by trunk, cooperative social systems.

## Tier 2 · Mechanism-dissection taxa

### Cephalopods
- common octopus and other well-studied octopus species
- cuttlefish where comparable cognitive data exist

Contrast: high behavioural flexibility and manipulation with short life and mostly nonhuman-like social architecture.

### Social insects
- Bombus terrestris
- honeybees
- selected ants / stingless bees when transmission data are suitable

Contrast: collective information processing, social learning and persistent colony structures without large individual brains.

### Pigeons
Contrast: experimental cumulative route improvement and social transmission.

### Cooperative breeders / teachers
- meerkats
- marmosets / tamarins where comparable prosocial and teaching evidence exists

Contrast: teaching or cooperative breeding without open-ended cumulative technology.

### Other primates
- capuchins
- macaques
- baboons

Contrast: tool use, innovation, traditions and variable social tolerance outside great apes.

## Tier 3 · Negative and calibration cases

Add taxa to cover low/high combinations of:
- brain investment;
- lifespan;
- group size;
- manipulation;
- vocal learning;
- environmental construction;
- social learning.

These cases prevent the model from learning only from famous "intelligent" species.

## Hominin time slices

Do not treat fossil taxa or archaeological phases as ordinary independent species. Use them as a separate temporal evidence layer with uncertainty.

Candidate units:
- Australopithecus / earliest tool-associated hominins where attribution is defensible;
- early Homo;
- Homo erectus / ergaster;
- Middle Pleistocene Homo;
- Neanderthals;
- early Homo sapiens;
- later Pleistocene / Holocene Homo sapiens.

Candidate proxies:
- brain volume and estimated energetics;
- hand morphology;
- tool-chain complexity;
- transport distance of materials;
- fire / food processing evidence;
- spatial organization;
- symbolic / representational artifacts;
- exchange-network proxies;
- demographic connectivity proxies.

The goal is event ordering: did a proposed necessary condition appear before, with, or after evidence of more open-ended accumulation?

## "Potential" output

A public-facing "bootstrap proximity" visualization may be produced only after validation. It should report module overlap and missing conditions with uncertainty, not a forecast that a living lineage will evolve human-like intelligence.
