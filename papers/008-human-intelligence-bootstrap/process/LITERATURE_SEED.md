# Literature seed · ARIS4C008

This is a seed map for the formal review, not a completed systematic review.

## Open-endedness as the target

Morgan, T. J. H. & Feldman, M. W. (2025). **Human culture is uniquely open-ended rather than uniquely cumulative.** *Nature Human Behaviour*, 9, 28–42.  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02035-y

Why it matters: cumulative change, transmission and even difficult socially acquired behaviours are not clean human/nonhuman boundaries. The stronger target is the breadth and open-ended expansion of inherited solutions.

## Nonhuman social acquisition beyond individual innovation

Bridges, A. D. et al. (2024). **Bumblebees socially learn behaviour too complex to innovate alone.** *Nature*, 627, 572–578.  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07126-4

van Leeuwen, E. J. C. et al. (2024). **Chimpanzees use social information to acquire a skill they fail to innovate.** *Nature Human Behaviour*, 8, 891–902.  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01836-5

Why they matter: "learning a behaviour one cannot innovate alone" cannot by itself be the sufficient explanation for human uniqueness.

## Defining cumulative culture carefully

Mesoudi, A. & Thornton, A. (2018). **What is cumulative cultural evolution?** *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6015846/

Dean, L. G. et al. (2014). **Human cumulative culture: a comparative perspective.** *Biological Reviews*, 89, 284–301.  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24033987/

Why they matter: cumulative culture is a family of phenomena rather than one yes/no Rubicon. ARIS4C008 therefore decomposes the outcome into traditions, ratcheting, breadth, recombination, externalization and open-endedness.

## Collective intelligence and network memory

Migliano, A. B. et al. (2022). **The origins of human cumulative culture: from the foraging niche to collective intelligence.** *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8666907/

Derex, M. & Mesoudi, A. (2020). **Cumulative Cultural Evolution within Evolving Population Structures.** *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*.  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32466991/

Why they matter: census group size is a poor stand-in for the culturally accessible information pool. Connectivity, cross-group exchange, specialization and distributed memory are explicit candidate mechanisms.

## Energetic constraints

Isler, K. & van Schaik, C. P. (2006). **Metabolic costs of brain size evolution.** *Biology Letters*.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1834002/

Herculano-Houzel, S. (2012). **The remarkable, yet not extraordinary, human brain as a scaled-up primate brain and its associated cost.** *PNAS*.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3386878/

Why they matter: the feasibility of large neuron numbers, long development and costly learning should be modeled rather than treated as background.

## Embodiment and manipulation

Kivell, T. L. (2015). **Evidence in hand: recent discoveries and the early evolution of human manual manipulation.** *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4614723/

Recent comparative work on primate thumb morphology and brain size provides an additional testable link between manipulation and cognitive investment:  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12381199/

Why it matters: cognition may need an effective physical interface to become cumulative material technology.

## Cetacean convergence

Fox, K. C. R., Muthukrishna, M. & Shultz, S. (2017). **The social and cultural roots of whale and dolphin brains.** *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, 1, 1699–1705.  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-017-0336-y

Why it matters: cetaceans provide an independent high-cognition/social-culture configuration under radically different bodily and environmental constraints.

## Cross-taxonomic culture infrastructure

Basava, K. et al. (2025). **Mapping nonhuman cultures with the Animal Culture Database.** *Scientific Data*, 12, 1019.  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05315-y

Why it matters: ACDB makes a source-traceable cross-species culture layer feasible, while also making research-effort bias measurable.

## Disagreement is part of the design

The literature does not establish teaching, imitation, language, joint attention, large population size, large brains, tool use, or long life as individually necessary and sufficient for open-ended cumulative intelligence. ARIS4C008 treats them as competing candidate components and actively searches for counterexamples.
