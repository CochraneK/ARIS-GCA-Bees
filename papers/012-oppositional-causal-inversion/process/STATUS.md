## Key design finding

The central insight is **index suppression**.

Statements that look like `X = O(X)` often expand into a causal relation such as:

`X[a1,l1,t1,d1,e1] -> mediator/feedback -> O(X)[a2,l2,t2,d2,e2]`

Once indices are explicit, the claim is no longer a logical contradiction. It becomes an ordinary causal hypothesis with measurable boundary conditions.

This insight itself is not enough for publication; the evidence map must show that the representation generalizes and adds predictive/classification value beyond existing domain theories.

## Current risks

1. **Renaming risk** — OCI may merely repackage paradox/unintended-consequence literatures.
2. **Semantic flexibility risk** — researchers could invent the "opposite" after seeing results.
3. **Cherry-picking risk** — famous paradoxes are selected precisely because they worked.
4. **Causal-quality risk** — many paradox claims may be observational.
5. **Cross-domain heterogeneity risk** — no meaningful common empirical parameter may exist.
6. **Political overinterpretation risk** — Orwellian examples can encourage normative or ideological claims unsupported by the causal evidence.
7. **Publication-format risk** — a general theory without systematic evidence would be too speculative.

## Locked safeguards

- Functional opposite must be prespecified and independently codeable.
- Actor/level/time/construct switches are mandatory fields.
- Evidence strength and normative desirability are coded separately.
- Counterevidence is required for each flagship domain.
- No grand pooled OCI effect size.
- War, threat, competition, deterrence, cohesion, regime support, and peace are separate constructs.
- Formal choice and effective autonomy are separate constructs.
- Selective information filtering and externally imposed ignorance are separate constructs.
- Political cases are descriptive/causal objects, not advocacy.
