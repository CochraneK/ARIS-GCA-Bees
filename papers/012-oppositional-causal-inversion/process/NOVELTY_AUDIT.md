# NOVELTY AUDIT — ARIS4C012

Updated: 2026-09-18

## Verdict

**Broad novelty claim: FAIL.**  
**Narrow indexed-representation claim: PROVISIONAL PASS.**

ARIS4C012 must not claim to discover that beneficial interventions can backfire, that local optimization can worsen global outcomes, or that complex systems generate unintended consequences. Those traditions are old, broad, and mature.

## Strongest umbrella competitors

### Merton (1936) — unanticipated consequences

Robert K. Merton's *The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action* already treated unanticipated consequences as a general problem spanning many forms of social action.

DOI: 10.2307/2084615

**Implication:** OCI cannot be sold as the first general science of counterintuitive consequences.

### Boudon (1977) — perverse effects / unintended social action

Raymond Boudon's *The Unintended Consequences of Social Action* explicitly develops perverse effects across social processes.

**Implication:** “good intention -> bad result” and micro-to-macro perverse outcomes are prior art.

### Perri 6 (2010) — typology of unintended/unanticipated consequences

The chapter *When Forethought and Outturn Part* argues that a single causal theory of all unintended consequences is unrealistic, but general conceptual frameworks, definitions, classifications, and typologies can still enable structured comparison.

DOI: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199573547.003.0003

**Implication:** even the idea of building a cross-case typology is not novel by itself.

### Perc (2026) — unintended consequences in complex adaptive systems

Matjaž Perc, *Unintended consequences of well-intended interventions*, PNAS.

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2533905122

The article explicitly links counterproductive intervention outcomes across ecological, technological, social, and economic systems to nonlinear dynamics, feedback, adaptive behavior, and multiple spatial/temporal scales. It emphasizes that local structural interventions can trigger functional reorganization and can make an adaptive system more resilient or harder to control.

**Implication:** cross-domain complex-systems feedback is already explicit in very recent literature. OCI cannot claim novelty merely from “feedback + cross-domain + different scales.”

### Rebound typology (2021)

The rebound literature already has a multi-level typology distinguishing mechanisms from effects across micro, meso, macro, and global levels and short- versus long-run time frames.

DOI: 10.1016/j.erss.2021.101982

**Implication:** level/time indexing itself is not globally novel.

### Safe-development systematic review (2024)

A systematic review of 94 studies documents risk-reduction interventions that can increase long-run risk via development, preparedness, risk perception, and rare-event mechanisms.

DOI: 10.1007/s11069-024-06774-z

**Implication:** well-developed domain literatures already quantify mechanism-specific reversals.

### Paradox theory

Smith & Lewis (2011) provide a general theory of persistent contradictory and interdependent organizational demands and dynamic responses.

DOI: 10.5465/amr.2009.0223

**Implication:** “contradictions across levels” is insufficient novelty.

## What remains potentially novel

OCI is defensible only as a **narrow methodological representation**, not as a newly discovered substantive law.

Candidate contribution:

> A machine-codeable representation for a restricted subset of counterintuitive consequences in which an exposure or intervention increases a **prespecified functional opposite**, with actor, level, time, construct, and environment indices explicitly restored and independently coded.

Three components must jointly matter:

1. **Prespecified functional opposition**  
   The “opposite” must be defined independently of the observed effect. This separates OCI from generic adverse or unintended consequences.

2. **Index restoration**  
   Apparent contradictions are rewritten with actor/level/time/construct/environment indices so that a slogan such as `X -> not-X` becomes a precise causal proposition.

3. **Empirical construct validation**  
   Independent coders must reliably distinguish OCI from neighboring paradox, side-effect, rebound, and unintended-consequence records. The representation must add classification or predictive value beyond domain labels and existing umbrella terminology.

If any of these fails, OCI should be demoted from a named framework to a descriptive coding scheme inside a systematic evidence map.

## Revised novelty ladder

### Claim level 0 — already known
Interventions can have unintended consequences.

### Claim level 1 — already known
Some consequences oppose intervention goals.

### Claim level 2 — already known in many domains
Feedback, adaptation, nonlinearities, cross-level effects, and temporal dynamics can generate backfire.

### Claim level 3 — candidate contribution
A common indexed notation can distinguish functional opposite-producing effects from ordinary unintended consequences.

### Claim level 4 — requires independent evidence
The notation can be applied reliably by independent coders across domains.

### Claim level 5 — requires out-of-domain validation
The indexed representation predicts mechanism or boundary-condition classes better than generic paradox/backfire labels or domain identity.

Only levels 3–5 belong to ARIS4C012's potential novelty.

## Title consequence

The phrase **“a science of”** is too broad given the prior art.

Recommended canonical title:

**When Opposites Become Causes: An Indexed Cross-Domain Framework for Oppositional Causal Inversion**

This keeps the conceptual hook while accurately presenting OCI as a proposed representation/framework.

## Publication consequence

The strongest realistic first paper is:

**theory + systematic evidence map + construct-validation study**

rather than a universal causal theory.

If independent coding is weak, publishable fallback:

**systematic taxonomy of opposite-producing causal claims and why rhetorical paradoxes fail scientific indexing.**
