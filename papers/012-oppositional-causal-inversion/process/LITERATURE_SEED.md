# LITERATURE SEED — ARIS4C012

Updated: 2026-09-18

This is a verified seed, not a saturated review.

## A. War / threat / conflict / security

### Huth (1999) — deterrence review
Paul K. Huth. "Deterrence and International Conflict: Empirical Findings and Theoretical Debates." *Annual Review of Political Science* 2:25-48. DOI: 10.1146/annurev.polisci.2.1.25

Use: deterrence is conditional on military balance, costly signals, reputation, and interests. This supports neither "war always prevents war" nor a simple monotonic force->peace claim.

### van Tartwijk et al. (2026) — intergroup competition meta-analysis
Tycho van Tartwijk, Angelo Romano, Leticia Micheli, Daniel Balliet. "Intergroup Competition Promotes Intragroup Cooperation: A Meta-Analysis of Social Dilemmas." *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, online 2026-08-31. DOI: 10.1177/01461672261476739

Use: preregistered meta-analysis; 25 articles, 70 effect sizes, N=6,051; medium positive overall effect (g=.677), stronger when intergroup competition includes interdependence. Strong seed for a level-switch mechanism, while still not equating cooperation with peace.

### Bornstein (2003) — intergroup conflict framework
Gary Bornstein. "Intergroup conflict: individual, group, and collective interests." *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 7(2):129-145. DOI: 10.1207/S15327957PSPR0702_129-145

Use: formalizes linked within-group and between-group social dilemmas.

### Morgan & Bickers (1992) — diversionary evidence caution
T. Clifton Morgan, Kenneth N. Bickers. "Domestic Discontent and the External Use of Force." *Journal of Conflict Resolution* 36(1):25-52.

Use: important counterweight; broad quantitative tests had not established a simple systematic domestic-discontent -> foreign-adventurism link, motivating more conditional formulations.

### Tir (2010) — conditional diversionary mechanism
Jaroslav Tir. "Territorial Diversion: Diversionary Theory of War and Territorial Conflict." *Journal of Politics* 72(2):413-425. DOI: 10.1017/S0022381609990879

Use: supports a more specific territorial-diversion mechanism and illustrates why boundary conditions matter.

### Tang (2009) / Glaser (2011) — security dilemma
Shiping Tang. "The Security Dilemma: A Conceptual Analysis." *Security Studies* 18:587-623. DOI: 10.1080/09636410903133050.

Use: a reverse seed: actions intended to increase security can make others less secure and feed back to reduce one's own security. This is closer to "security -> insecurity" than "war -> peace."

## B. Freedom / choice / autonomy

### Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015) — choice overload
"Choice overload: A conceptual review and meta-analysis." *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 25(2):333-358. DOI: 10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002

Use: 99 observations, N=7,202. Choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and decision goal moderate overload.

### Mazmanian, Orlikowski & Yates (2013) — autonomy paradox
"The Autonomy Paradox: The Implications of Mobile Email Devices for Knowledge Professionals." *Organization Science* 24(5):1337-1357. DOI: 10.1287/orsc.1120.0806

Use: short-run flexibility/control associated with collective norms of continual connectivity and diminished ability to disconnect.

### Ng et al. (2012) — autonomy counterevidence
"Self-Determination Theory Applied to Health Contexts: A Meta-Analysis." *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 7(4):325-340. DOI: 10.1177/1745691612447309

Use: 184 independent datasets; autonomous motivation and need satisfaction relate positively to beneficial health outcomes. This prevents a global "freedom is harmful" interpretation.

### Hagger & McAnally Star (2026) — workplace SDT counterevidence
"Self-Determination Theory and Workplace Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis." *Stress and Health* 42(1):e70151. DOI: 10.1002/smi.70151

Use: contemporary counterevidence showing adaptive workplace associations via need satisfaction and autonomous motivation, with moderators.

## C. Ignorance / selective information / information load

### Gigerenzer (2009) and ecological rationality
Gerd Gigerenzer. "Homo Heuristicus: Why Biased Minds Make Better Inferences." *Topics in Cognitive Science*. DOI: 10.1111/j.1756-8765.2008.01006.x

Use: less-is-more effects can arise because of bias-variance tradeoffs and environmental structure. Supports selective-information, not generalized ignorance.

### Hertwig & Engel (2016)
"Homo Ignorans: Deliberately Choosing Not to Know." *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 11(3):359-372. DOI: 10.1177/1745691616635594

Use: defines deliberate ignorance and reviews its functions and normative heterogeneity.

### Sweeny et al. (2010)
"Information Avoidance: Who, What, When, and Why." *Review of General Psychology* 14(4):340-353. DOI: 10.1037/a0021288

Use: separates information avoidance from related phenomena and catalogs motivations; important adverse/neutral comparator.

### Sims (2003)
"Implications of Rational Inattention." *Journal of Monetary Economics* 50(3):665-690. DOI: 10.1016/S0304-3932(03)00029-1

Use: finite information-processing capacity as a formal constraint.

### Morris & Shin (2002)
"Social Value of Public Information." *American Economic Review* 92(5):1521-1534. DOI: 10.1257/000282802762024610

Use: when agents possess independent private information and have coordination motives, the welfare effect of more public information can be ambiguous.

### King, Pan & Roberts (2013)
"How Censorship in China Allows Government Criticism but Silences Collective Expression." *American Political Science Review* 107(2):326-343. DOI: 10.1017/S0003055413000014

Use: large-scale evidence that the studied censorship system disproportionately targeted collective-expression/mobilization content rather than criticism per se. Candidate power-asymmetry evidence. Do not infer from this paper alone that censorship generally strengthens regimes or that ignorance benefits citizens.

## D. Closest umbrella theories

### Smith & Lewis (2011) — paradox theory
"Toward a Theory of Paradox: A Dynamic Equilibrium Model of Organizing." *Academy of Management Review* 36(2):381-403. DOI: 10.5465/amr.2009.0223

Use: major neighboring framework for persistent, interdependent contradictions. OCI must show why indexed causal reversal is a narrower/different object.

### Schumpe et al. (2018) — counterfinality
"Counterfinality: On the Increased Perceived Instrumentality of Means to a Goal." *Frontiers in Psychology* 9:1052. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01052

Use: a means that harms an alternative goal can be perceived as more instrumental to a focal goal. Neighboring means-goal framework, but primarily about perceived instrumentality rather than general cross-domain causal sign reversal.

## Seed conclusion

The seed supports **conditional reversals and cross-index effects**, not literal Orwellian identities.

The strongest immediate move is:

> apparent contradiction -> restore indices -> specify mediator/feedback -> test boundary conditions -> compare with counterevidence.

Novelty remains provisional until the umbrella-theory search is saturated.


## E. Saturation / umbrella competitors added after Pilot 0A

### Merton (1936)
**The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action.** *American Sociological Review* 1(6):894–904. DOI: 10.2307/2084615.

Use: foundational umbrella prior art; rejects any claim that OCI discovers unintended consequences as a general phenomenon.

### Perri 6 (2010)
**When Forethought and Outturn Part: Types of Unanticipated and Unintended Consequences.** DOI: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199573547.003.0003.

Use: direct methodological competitor. Explicitly favors conceptual frameworks and typologies over one universal causal theory.

### Perc (2026)
**Unintended consequences of well-intended interventions.** *PNAS* 123(2):e2533905122. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2533905122.

Use: recent cross-domain complex-systems umbrella covering feedback, adaptation, nonlinearities, and scale. Strongly narrows OCI novelty.

### Abrams (2009)
**When does greater mortality increase population size? The long history and diverse mechanisms underlying the hydra effect.** *Ecology Letters* 12(5):462–474. DOI: 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01282.x.

Use: clean ecological dynamic reversal benchmark.

### Li & Shi (2026)
**Message effects on psychological reactance: meta-analyses.** *Human Communication Research* 52(1):38–52. DOI: 10.1093/hcr/hqaf016.

Use: meta-analytic evidence that freedom-threatening messaging raises reactance, which is negatively related to persuasion outcomes. Candidate control/persuasion-backfire family.

### Jansen & Martin (2015)
**The Streisand Effect and Censorship Backfire.** *International Journal of Communication* 9:656–671.

Use: dissemination-suppression attempts can sometimes amplify attention/outrage; candidate actor/network-feedback family, not a universal censorship claim.

### John et al. (2023)
**Dead rats, dopamine, performance metrics, and peacock tails: Proxy failure is an inherent risk in goal-oriented systems.** *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*. DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X23002753.

Use: broad proxy-failure competitor adjacent to Goodhart/Campbell effects.
