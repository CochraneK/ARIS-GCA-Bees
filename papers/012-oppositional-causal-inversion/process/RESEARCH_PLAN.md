# RESEARCH PLAN — ARIS4C012

## 0. State

**ARIS conditional GO · theory/evidence-map phase.**

Do not attempt a single pooled effect across war, autonomy, information, and organizational paradoxes. The first task is construct validation and systematic mapping.

## 1. Main research question

> Across scientific domains, under what recurring and prespecifiable conditions does increasing a construct produce a causal increase in a functional opposite after an explicit actor, level, time, construct, feedback, or capacity index changes?

## 2. Secondary questions

1. Which index switches recur most often?
2. Which mechanism families have the strongest causal evidence?
3. How often are published "paradoxes" only semantic/indexing artifacts?
4. Do reversals become predictable from moderators, thresholds, feedback, and environmental structure?
5. Does one common representation improve prediction/classification beyond domain-specific labels?
6. Are the Orwellian slogans special cases of a broader family or merely rhetorically similar but scientifically unrelated?

## 3. Phase A — closest-prior-work audit

Search neighboring umbrella theories before claiming novelty:

- paradox theory;
- unintended/perverse consequences;
- boomerang effects;
- iatrogenic effects;
- counterfinality;
- security dilemma;
- ecological rationality / less-is-more;
- rational inattention;
- deliberate ignorance;
- choice overload;
- autonomy paradox;
- externalities and equilibrium feedback;
- risk compensation / Peltzman-type effects;
- Goodhart/Campbell-like target distortion;
- Braess-like network paradoxes;
- rebound/Jevons effects;
- antifragility and robustness-through-constraint.

Output: prior-art matrix with construct definition, mechanism, level, causal direction, empirical domain, and why it does/does not subsume OCI.

## 4. Phase B — systematic evidence map

### 4.1 Databases

Primary:
- OpenAlex;
- Crossref;
- PubMed where relevant;
- discipline-specific publisher/index searches.

Optional validation:
- Scopus/Web of Science if legitimately available.

### 4.2 Search families

Use broad neutral families rather than Orwell-specific queries:

- paradox* AND causal/effect/outcome;
- backfire / boomerang / counterproductive / self-defeating;
- unintended / perverse consequence;
- iatrogenic;
- less-is-more;
- overload / too much / excessive choice/information/control;
- security dilemma / deterrence;
- counterfinality;
- rational inattention;
- deliberate ignorance / information avoidance;
- rebound / compensation;
- overoptimization / fragility.

The Orwell phrases are used only as supplementary citation/semantic searches.

### 4.3 Inclusion

Include empirical or formal-theoretical work that:
- identifies an exposure/intervention X;
- defines an outcome plausibly opposed to X or to X's intended function;
- proposes a causal path or allows causal identification assessment;
- provides enough information to code actor/level/time/construct indices.

### 4.4 Exclusion

Exclude:
- pure literary commentary without an empirical/formal mechanism;
- mere lexical paradox;
- simultaneous contradictory labels with no causal path;
- post-hoc "opposite" definitions chosen solely because an effect was surprising;
- ordinary negative side effects that are unrelated to the focal construct or goal;
- causal-direction-reversal errors where "causal inversion" means mistaking Y->X for X->Y.

## 5. Phase C — double coding and construct validation

At least two independent coding passes for:
- opposition validity;
- index-switch type;
- mechanism family;
- evidence strength;
- causal identification;
- boundary conditions.

Report agreement before adjudication.

A candidate universal taxonomy is rejected or revised if coders cannot apply it reliably.

## 6. Phase D — quantitative synthesis by compatible family

Meta-analysis is permitted only within sufficiently homogeneous families.

Candidate first syntheses:
- intergroup competition -> intragroup cooperation;
- choice overload under prespecified moderators;
- autonomy-support -> adaptive outcomes as counterevidence;
- less-is-more heuristic comparisons;
- information-avoidance outcomes where comparable.

Do not calculate a grand "OCI effect size."

## 7. Phase E — corpus-scale discovery

Build an abstract/title corpus and search for linguistic markers such as:
- paradox;
- backfire;
- self-defeating;
- counterproductive;
- boomerang;
- less is more;
- unintended consequence;
- excessive;
- overload;
- reversal.

Use a human-reviewed classifier to distinguish:
1. true indexed causal reversal;
2. trade-off;
3. ordinary side effect;
4. rhetorical paradox;
5. causal-direction error;
6. non-causal association.

Estimate prevalence with uncertainty and audit classifier error.

## 8. Confirmatory hypotheses

### H1 — indexing hypothesis
A large share of apparently self-contradictory causal claims become logically coherent after actor/level/time/construct indices are restored.

### H2 — moderator hypothesis
Credible reversals are concentrated in identifiable moderator regimes rather than showing universal opposite-direction effects.

### H3 — feedback hypothesis
Dynamic/equilibrium and capacity mechanisms explain more robust reversals than pure lexical or rhetorical paradox categories.

### H4 — predictability hypothesis
A model using index-switch and mechanism features predicts the sign/boundary of reversal out of domain better than a baseline using only the domain label.

### H5 — heterogeneity hypothesis
The Orwell triad will map onto multiple distinct mechanisms; any common theory must operate at the representation level rather than claim one shared substantive mechanism.

## 9. Key falsifiers

Reject or sharply narrow the general theory if:
- inter-rater reliability for "functional opposite" is poor;
- index restoration explains rhetoric but adds no empirical predictive value;
- mechanism classes are domain-specific and non-transferable;
- causal evidence is too weak or biased toward novelty language;
- predicted moderator/threshold patterns do not replicate;
- corpus discovery mostly returns ordinary trade-offs rather than OCI cases.

## 10. Seed evidence already located

- Intergroup competition -> intragroup cooperation: 2026 preregistered meta-analysis, 25 articles, 70 effects, N=6,051, medium positive overall effect; interdependence moderates the effect.
- Deterrence: established review literature supports conditional success/failure rather than a universal war-prevents-war rule.
- Choice overload: meta-analysis of 99 observations, N=7,202, with complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and decision goal as moderators.
- Autonomy paradox: qualitative/organizational evidence that short-term mobile-work flexibility can produce collective norms of constant availability and reduced ability to disconnect.
- Autonomy counterevidence: SDT meta-analytic literature generally links autonomy support/need satisfaction with adaptive outcomes.
- Less-is-more heuristics: established ecological-rationality literature documents environments in which ignoring information improves accuracy.
- Deliberate ignorance: theoretical/review literature identifies functions and contexts of intentionally not knowing.
- Public information: Morris-Shin coordination model shows welfare effects of more public information can be ambiguous when agents also hold private information.
- Rational inattention: formal economics models finite information-processing capacity.
- Information control/collective action: empirical political-science work documents selective censorship aimed at collective-expression/mobilization content; this is evidence for a possible power-asymmetry mechanism, not direct proof that "ignorance creates strength."

## 11. Immediate execution queue

1. Freeze the concept schema and candidate taxonomy.
2. Build closest-prior-work matrix.
3. Expand seed to >=50 high-relevance sources across >=8 domains.
4. Draft reproducible search strings and screening form.
5. Run a 30-paper double-coding Pilot 0.
6. Measure coder agreement and revise the taxonomy.
7. Only then preregister/freeze the full evidence-map protocol.
8. After mapping, select at most 2-4 quantitatively synthesizable families.
