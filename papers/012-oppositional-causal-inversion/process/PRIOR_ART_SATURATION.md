# PRIOR-ART SATURATION AUDIT — ARIS4C012

Updated: 2026-09-18

## Purpose

Stress-test whether Oppositional Causal Inversion (OCI) is genuinely a new theoretical class or only a relabeling of established unintended-consequence, paradox, rebound, and backfire traditions.

## Result

**Broad inversion/backfire novelty: rejected.**  
**Restricted indexed-representation novelty: still testable.**

The search found mature prior art across sociology, complex systems, energy economics, transportation, ecology, psychology, communication, clinical science, scientometrics, and risk science.

## Umbrella frameworks

### 1. Unanticipated consequences — sociology

**Merton, R. K. (1936).** The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action. *American Sociological Review*, 1(6), 894–904.  
DOI: 10.2307/2084615

Relevance: establishes unanticipated consequences as a general social-scientific problem long before modern "backfire" terminology.

### 2. Perverse effects — sociology

**Boudon, R. (1977).** *The Unintended Consequences of Social Action.*

Relevance: explicitly treats perverse social effects, including cases in which purposive action produces adverse aggregate outcomes.

### 3. Typology over universal theory

**Perri 6 (2010).** When Forethought and Outturn Part: Types of Unanticipated and Unintended Consequences.  
DOI: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199573547.003.0003

Relevance: argues that a single causal theory of all unintended consequences is unrealistic while clear definitions and typologies can support structured comparison. This is a direct warning against overclaiming OCI as a universal substantive law.

### 4. Complex adaptive systems

**Perc, M. (2026).** Unintended consequences of well-intended interventions. *PNAS*, 123(2), e2533905122.  
DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2533905122

Relevance: already integrates nonlinear dynamics, feedback, adaptive behavior, local/global mismatch, and multiple spatial/temporal scales across ecological, technological, social, and economic systems.

Implication: "cross-domain + feedback + scale switching" is not sufficient novelty.

## Strong domain benchmark families

### 5. Rebound / Jevons

**The Jevons paradox unravelled: A multi-level typology of rebound effects and mechanisms** (2021). *Energy Research & Social Science*, 74, 101982.  
DOI: 10.1016/j.erss.2021.101982

The typology separates mechanisms from effect magnitudes across micro, meso, macro, and global levels and short- versus long-run time frames.

Implication: multilevel and temporal indexing already exists in a mature domain-specific framework.

### 6. Braess / network paradoxes

**Bibliometric analysis and systematic literature review of the traffic paradoxes (1968–2022)** (2023). *Transportation Research Part B*, 177, 102832.  
DOI: 10.1016/j.trb.2023.102832

Relevance: formal benchmark where adding a network link can increase equilibrium travel cost.

Implication: one of the clearest OCI-compatible examples, but not a discovery of OCI.

### 7. Safe-development paradox

**Fusinato et al. (2024).** Safe development paradox: evidence and methodological insights from a systematic review. *Natural Hazards*, 120, 13693–13714.  
DOI: 10.1007/s11069-024-06774-z

The review covered 94 studies and reported three recurring mechanisms: development in protected areas, reduced preparedness/false sense of safety, and rare-event damage.

Implication: domain literature already supports temporal/feedback reversal with explicit mechanisms.

### 8. Hydra effect — ecology

**Abrams, P. A. (2009).** When does greater mortality increase population size? The long history and diverse mechanisms underlying the hydra effect. *Ecology Letters*, 12(5), 462–474.  
DOI: 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01282.x

Relevance: increased mortality can under particular density-dependent dynamics increase population size.

Implication: unusually clean same-domain dynamic reversal; important benchmark for testing whether OCI can represent nonlinear dynamic feedback without relying on semantic construct switching.

### 9. Psychological reactance — communication/psychology

**Li & Shi (2026).** Message effects on psychological reactance: meta-analyses. *Human Communication Research*, 52(1), 38–52.  
DOI: 10.1093/hcr/hqaf016

Across 28 articles, 33 studies, and 146 effect sizes, high freedom-threatening language increased anger, negative cognitions, and reactance; reactance-related outcomes were associated with poorer persuasion outcomes.

Implication: a well-developed mechanism by which stronger control/persuasion attempts can undermine persuasion, with identifiable moderators.

### 10. Boomerang effects — behavior change

**Kuang et al. (2020).** Do descriptive norms messaging interventions backfire? Protocol for a systematic review of the boomerang effect. *Systematic Reviews*, 9, 267.  
DOI: 10.1186/s13643-020-01533-0

Relevance: directly defines subgroup-specific movement toward undesirable behavior after a behavior-change message.

Implication: OCI must distinguish "opposite of intended outcome" from a generic adverse side effect.

### 11. Iatrogenic psychological intervention

**Lilienfeld, S. O. (2007).** Psychological Treatments That Cause Harm. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 2(1), 53–70.  
DOI: 10.1111/j.1745-6916.2007.00029.x

Relevance: treatment can worsen the condition it intends to improve.

Implication: a strong intervention-target reversal family with substantial identification challenges.

### 12. Censorship backfire / Streisand effect

**Jansen & Martin (2015).** The Streisand Effect and Censorship Backfire. *International Journal of Communication*, 9, 656–671.

Relevance: attempts to reduce dissemination can, in some circumstances, increase attention or outrage.

Implication: actor/network feedback family; it should not be generalized into a political claim about censorship always failing.

### 13. Goodhart / proxy failure

**Fire & Guestrin (2019).** Over-optimization of academic publishing metrics: observing Goodhart's Law in action. *GigaScience*, 8(6), giz053.  
DOI: 10.1093/gigascience/giz053

**John et al. (2023).** Dead rats, dopamine, performance metrics, and peacock tails: Proxy failure is an inherent risk in goal-oriented systems. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*.  
DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X23002753

Relevance: optimizing proxy measures can degrade proxy validity or distort the underlying objective.

Implication: construct substitution + adaptive feedback is already a recognized general problem.

## What the saturation audit rules out

ARIS4C012 must not claim novelty for any of the following alone:

- interventions can backfire;
- beneficial actions can have harmful side effects;
- local optimization can worsen global outcomes;
- more can sometimes be worse;
- nonlinear feedback can reverse effects;
- short-run and long-run effects can differ;
- micro and macro effects can differ;
- adaptive agents can defeat interventions;
- paradoxes occur across disciplines;
- a cross-domain taxonomy of unintended consequences is itself unprecedented.

## Remaining candidate contribution

A defensible OCI contribution requires **all three**:

### A. Prespecified functional opposition
The "opposite" must be defined independently of the focal effect. Generic harm is not enough.

### B. Explicit index restoration
Every candidate is represented as:

`X[a,l,t,d,e] -> M/feedback -> O(X)[a',l',t',d',e']`

so actor, level, time, construct, and environment switching cannot remain hidden in rhetoric.

### C. Independent construct validation
Independent coders must reliably distinguish strict OCI from:

- ordinary unintended consequences;
- generic adverse side effects;
- paradoxical coexistence;
- trade-offs;
- reverse causality;
- proxy failure without a functional opposite;
- null/counterevidence;
- purely rhetorical paradox.

## Novelty decision rule

### Strong framework claim permitted only if
1. opposition-validity coding is independently reliable;
2. OCI candidacy is independently reliable;
3. mechanism classes show acceptable reliability after explicit definitions;
4. the indexed representation distinguishes OCI from broad unintended-consequence labels on an unseen sample;
5. at least one out-of-domain validation shows classification or predictive value.

### Otherwise
Reframe the paper as:

**A systematic evidence map and taxonomy of functional-opposite-producing causal claims across scientific domains.**

This fallback remains publishable and avoids inventing a universal theory where the literature supports only family resemblance.
