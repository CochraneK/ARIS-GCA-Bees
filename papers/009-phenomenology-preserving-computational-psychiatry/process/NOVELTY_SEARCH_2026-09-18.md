# ARIS4C009 · Targeted novelty search log

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Purpose:** stress-test the narrow novelty claim before a full systematic novelty review.

## Claim being tested

Not: “computational phenomenology is new.” That claim is false.

The narrower candidate claim is:

> Psychiatric representations can be benchmarked as lossy encodings of the same source episodes, with source-grounded phenomenological distortion measured separately from reliability, biological/construct validity, prediction and acquisition burden; representations can then be compared on a multi-objective fidelity–burden frontier.

## Query families

Targeted web/PubMed-style searches combined terms including:

- "rate distortion" + psychiatry / psychopathology / phenomenology;
- "phenomenology preserving" + computational psychiatry;

## Closest prior work

### Computational phenomenology / generative passages

Ramstead, Seth, Hesp and colleagues formalize descriptions of lived experience with generative modeling.

**Overlap:** explicit formalization of phenomenology.  
**Difference:** no same-episode clinical representation benchmark with source-grounded distortion and burden as separate axes.

### Generative neurophenomenology

Monnier, Adel & Dumas (2025), PMID 41458363, DOI 10.1093/nc/niaf052.

**Overlap:** integrates first-person, third-person, interpersonal and computational methods and explicitly recognizes information loss in communicating rich subjective experience.  
**Difference:** not an explicit psychiatric representation-compression benchmark or Pareto fidelity frontier.

### EASE versus self-report substitution

Cobanovic et al. (2025), PMID 40245844, DOI 10.1159/000545364.

**Overlap:** directly asks whether cheaper self-rating representations preserve the same psychopathological construct.  
**Difference:** validity review rather than a general cross-representation distortion framework.

### IPASE versus EASE empirical comparison

Henriksen et al. (2026), PMID 42044094, DOI 10.1159/000552007.

**Overlap:** unusually direct evidence of representation-dependent loss/misclassification. IPASE and EASE correlate only moderately and qualitative interpretation differs.  
**Difference:** one construct and two instruments; no general source → multiple-representation benchmark and no explicit burden frontier.

### Phenomenological fidelity concept

Hurlburt and colleagues distinguish phenomenological fidelity from conventional validity in first-person methods.

**Overlap:** the epistemic concern and term already exist.  
**Difference:** 009 operationalizes fidelity as a multi-component source-grounded psychiatric benchmark and connects it to computational multi-objective comparison.

## Negative evidence from targeted search

Exact or near-exact searches for:

- “fidelity frontier” in psychiatry/psychopathology;
- phenomenological “rate distortion” in psychiatry;
- psychiatric “representation loss” benchmark;
- phenomenology-preserving computational psychiatry;

did not surface a mature framework matching the complete proposed design.

Searches did surface unrelated lossy-compression work in medical imaging and semantic-fidelity terminology in machine learning. These are conceptual analogues, not prior psychiatric implementations.

## Current novelty judgment

**Provisional narrow novelty is defensible.**

Novelty should be claimed only for the benchmarking architecture, not for computational phenomenology, neurophenomenology, EASE/EAWE/STEP, computational psychiatry, idiographic dynamics or information theory itself.

## What would invalidate the novelty claim?

A prior framework that already combines most of:

1. same underlying psychopathological episodes;
2. multiple progressively compressed psychiatric representations;
3. source-grounded semantic/relational/context/temporal distortion;
4. reliability and external validity kept separate;
5. explicit burden/rate measurement;
6. Pareto or rate-distortion comparison.

If found, 009 becomes a replication/extension rather than an originality claim.

## Remaining systematic gate

This is **not** a PRISMA-style systematic review.

Before a manuscript makes a strong novelty claim, search at minimum:

- PubMed/MEDLINE;
- PsycINFO;
- Scopus or Web of Science;
- PhilPapers or equivalent philosophy databases;
- backward/forward citation chains from computational phenomenology, EASE self-report validity and phenomenological-fidelity papers.

Record full strings, dates, result counts and screening decisions.
