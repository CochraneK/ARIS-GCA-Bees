# AUTO REVIEW — ARIS4C005

## Review stage

Pre-data adversarial review after idea saturation, estimand definition, causal-model drafting, and pilot planning.

## Overall assessment

**High-potential and unusually distinctive, but only if the project resists the temptation to produce one spectacular global number.**

The strongest publishable contribution is a measurement-and-propagation framework that distinguishes detected corrections from latent severe integrity failures, calibrates article-level detection, and quantifies downstream dependence in natural units.

The human-time and innovation modules are valuable, but they must remain subordinate to measurement validity. Never-Woken Sleeping Beauties should be treated as an upside exploratory analysis, not the load-bearing claim.

Overall gate: **GO, with several blocking conditions before any global hidden-burden estimate is published.**

---

## Major concern 1 — the project is large enough to become several papers

The current scope contains:

- global bibliometrics;
- latent-class measurement;
- manual research-integrity audit;
- citation semantics;
- clinical evidence synthesis;
- grant accounting;
- researcher time accounting;
- career spillovers;
- causal topic dynamics;
- Sleeping Beauty modeling.

A single manuscript may become impossible to validate or review coherently.

### Required fix

Treat ARIS4C005 as a **program with a core paper plus extensions**.

Core-paper minimum:

1. global denominator + detected lower bound;
2. calibrated article-level hidden-burden pilot;
3. direct downstream material-dependence / evidence-contamination module;
4. one defensible human or financial burden module.

Innovation-delay and Sleeping Beauty results can become companion papers if they mature.

**Gate: GO — architecture already reflects modularity; enforce at manuscript stage.**

---

## Major concern 2 — retraction is a governance outcome, not a prevalence sample

Papers are more likely to be corrected when they are prominent, scrutinized, machine-readable, investigated by responsive institutions, or published by correction-active journals/publishers.

### Why this matters

A country, journal, or field can have more retractions because it detects problems better, not because it has more underlying problems.

### Required fix

- never interpret raw retraction rates as true misconduct prevalence;
- model scrutiny/correction capacity;
- use random article audits independent of retraction status;
- report detected rates and latent prevalence as separate results;
- do not publish country “fraud rankings.”

**Gate: BLOCKING for any prevalence claim; already encoded in causal model.**

---

## Major concern 3 — the latent model may be statistically unidentified

Multiple detectors can all be correlated manifestations of the same paper-mill process, and most detectors lack known sensitivity/specificity in the target population.

A flexible five-state model could return elegant posterior intervals driven almost entirely by priors.

### Required fix

- binary severe-failure latent state for primary model;
- population-random manual audit anchor;
- detector-positive oversamples only as calibration supplements;
- posterior/prior information-gain diagnostics;
- conditional-dependence sensitivity;
- publish failure-to-identify if the data cannot support the model.

**Gate: BLOCKING until Pilot B/C demonstrates information beyond priors.**

---

## Major concern 4 — “manual gold standard” is not truly gold

Research misconduct often depends on intent, original data, institutional investigations, and information unavailable from a paper alone. Expert reviewers can identify anomalies without being able to establish misconduct.

### Required fix

The audit must adjudicate **evidence of severe scientific-integrity failure**, not legal guilt.

Use labels including:

- severe supported;
- serious unresolved;
- honest major error;
- minor/immaterial;
- no material problem found;
- indeterminate.

Do not publish person-level accusations generated solely by article audit/model scores.

**Gate: BLOCKING until adjudication protocol is frozen.**

---

## Major concern 5 — misconduct and unreliable scientific content are orthogonal axes

Plagiarism is misconduct but may leave a scientific finding correct. Honest error can invalidate a finding without misconduct. Fake peer review can compromise publication integrity without proving fabricated data.

### Required fix

Maintain separate exposure outputs:

- E1-S: severe scientific unreliability with integrity evidence;
- E1-M: confirmed major misconduct;
- E1-P: publication-process integrity failure;
- E3: broader unreliability/waste without misconduct evidence.

Downstream epistemic-cost analyses should prioritize E1-S, not every misconduct reason.

**Gate: GO — added to `EXPOSURE_CODEBOOK.md`.**

---

## Major concern 6 — Researcher-Life-Years can become fake precision

There is no universal “time required to produce one paper.” Team size, study design, infrastructure, field, failed experiments, grant structure, and overlapping outputs vary by orders of magnitude.

### Required fix

- never set one paper = one researcher-year in the empirical model;
- build study-type effort distributions from external data/case reconstruction;
- separate direct production, follow-up, verification, and correction time;
- show scenario ranges until calibration is credible;
- report sensitivity to effort assumptions prominently.

**Gate: BLOCKING for scaled RLY; scenario engine is allowed now because it is explicitly non-empirical.**

---

## Major concern 7 — citation is not contamination

Many papers cite retracted work to criticize it, discuss research integrity, provide historical context, or use a method that remains valid.

### Required fix

Custom citation-dependence labels and human validation. Primary contamination requires substantive result/data dependence or evidence-synthesis inclusion.

High precision should be prioritized over recall.

**Gate: BLOCKING before global SCF claims.**

---

## Major concern 8 — second-order propagation creates attribution/double-counting problems

One downstream work may depend on multiple problematic papers, and one bad paper may enter many paths to the same later node.

### Required fix

Report both:

- source-level paths;
- unique affected downstream works.

For burden attribution use marginal/path-aware rules rather than assigning the full downstream object to every problematic ancestor.

**Gate: GO with anti-double-counting implementation required.**

---

## Major concern 9 — retraction timing is endogenous

Papers may be retracted when scrutiny or citations surge. Citations naturally decay with age. A naive before/after plot can falsely attribute ordinary aging or scrutiny dynamics to the correction.

### Required fix

- matched controls with similar pre-event trajectories;
- event-time normalization;
- article-age/field-time controls;
- critique vs reliance citations;
- placebo event dates;
- survival/rate models.

Knowledge Ghost Half-Life is descriptive unless this design performs well.

**Gate: BLOCKING for causal “retraction caused decay” language.**

---

## Major concern 10 — whole grants cannot be called wasted

A grant can support many valid publications, data resources, trainees, and discoveries even if one output is retracted.

### Required fix

- associated grant dollars = funding exposure/context;
- attributable cost uses explicit allocation rules;
- show multiple allocation methods;
- never compute `global R&D × misconduct rate` as burden.

**Gate: GO — already encoded; manuscript must preserve it.**

---

## Major concern 11 — participant “sacrifice” risks mixing ethics and misconduct

Nonpublication can waste participant contributions without any fraud. Conversely, a retracted trial may still produce reusable knowledge about methods/adverse events.

### Required fix

- E1/E2 invalidated trials separate from E3 nonpublication/discontinuation;
- participant count, time, and risk are separate quantities;
- do not imply every unpublished participant was physically harmed;
- phrase the outcome as contribution/burden without usable knowledge gain.

**Gate: GO with clinical-subset framing.**

---

## Major concern 12 — innocent-collaborator penalties are not automatically social welfare loss

Some citation decline after a misconduct event may reflect rational updating about shared methods/lab quality rather than unjust stigma.

### Required fix

Call the primary outcome **collateral career effect**, not automatically “unfair harm.”

Where possible distinguish:

- collaborators involved in the problematic work;
- uninvolved prior collaborators;
- independent work before/after the collaboration;
- methodological/lab dependence.

**Gate: GO with neutral effect language.**

---

## Major concern 13 — Innovation Delay is vulnerable to reverse causality and topic-choice bias

Unreliable findings may arise precisely in fast-moving, difficult, high-value topics where funding and attention would have been volatile anyway.

### Required fix

- topic/control matching only on pre-event information;
- pre-trend diagnostics;
- placebo shocks;
- synthetic controls for major cases;
- multiple topic definitions;
- primary innovation estimand = delay, not permanent disappearance.

**Gate: BLOCKING before quasi-causal IDY claims.**

---

## Major concern 14 — “missing good papers” may not be observable as publication displacement

Scientific publishing has flexible capacity; a fraudulent article does not mechanically consume a slot that would otherwise go to a good article.

### Required fix

Operationalize crowding-out through mechanisms that can be observed:

- funding share;
- attention/citation share;
- researcher entry;
- time-to-alternative uptake;
- editorial/reviewer capacity where submission data exist.

Do not report “one bad paper displaced one good paper.”

**Gate: GO after mechanistic definition.**

---

## Major concern 15 — Never-Woken Sleeping Beauties are fundamentally unobserved counterfactuals

A paper that never receives recognition may be excellent, irrelevant, wrong, incomprehensible, superseded, or simply unlucky. There is no label for “would have been a landmark but for bad science.”

### Required fix

Inference order:

1. reproduce observed Sleeping Beauty definitions;
2. temporally validate awakening prediction;
3. estimate awakening delay among eventual awakeners;
4. only then model expected permanent non-awakening.

Results must remain expected counts under structural assumptions. Do not identify individual masterpieces.

**Gate: EXPLORATORY only.**

---

## Major concern 16 — cross-field detector validity may differ dramatically

Image manipulation tests matter in wet-lab biomedicine but not mathematics. Statistical consistency tools apply only to specific reported structures. Paper-mill signals may be language/genre dependent.

### Required fix

- applicability mask for every detector;
- field-specific calibration where data support it;
- broad-field partial pooling, not forced identical detector behavior;
- report detector coverage denominators;
- allow “not applicable,” not just negative.

**Gate: BLOCKING for global latent model until coverage/missingness is modeled.**

---

## Major concern 17 — temporal nonstationarity, especially generative AI, may break historical models

The mechanisms of low-integrity publication are changing rapidly. Paper mills, manipulated peer review, and generative-AI content create detector regimes unlike those of 2000–2015.

### Required fix

- publication-period hierarchy;
- train/test splits by time;
- current AI-related reasons treated as separate features/mechanisms;
- avoid assuming one stable prevalence/detector process over 2000–2025;
- annual trend claims include correction lag.

**Gate: GO with temporal modeling.**

---

## Major concern 18 — a public repository must not become an accusation database

Publishing article-level model scores linked to author identities could unfairly stigmatize researchers when the model is probabilistic and detector errors are inevitable.

### Required fix

- publish aggregate estimates and validated public correction records;
- do not publicly label unretracted named papers/authors as fraudulent based solely on model probability;
- release derived detector data only after legal/ethical review and with uncertainty/context;
- use de-identified/sample IDs for high-risk adjudication outputs where appropriate.

**Gate: BLOCKING for public release of article-level E2 predictions.**

---

## Major concern 19 — double-counting can make the burden vector look larger than reality

Grant salary, researcher-years, review hours, follow-up costs, contaminated papers, and innovation delay may represent overlapping manifestations of the same event.

### Required fix

Keep natural units separate and enforce the anti-double-counting rules in `LOSS_ONTOLOGY.md`. Do not sum the dashboard into a grand monetary or points total.

**Gate: GO — already frozen.**

---

## Major concern 20 — vivid communication can outrun inferential certainty

“Millions of years of scientific youth,” “tens of thousands of careers,” or “thousands of lost Sleeping Beauties” are memorable but can become misleading if scenario assumptions are mistaken for measured facts.

### Required fix

Every vivid conversion carries one of:

- empirical estimate + uncertainty;
- calibrated model estimate;
- explicitly labeled scenario.

The public story must visually separate these states.

**Gate: BLOCKING at publication/story review.**

---

# Minor concerns

1. OpenAlex work-type changes/reclassification require snapshot/version preservation.
2. Crossref/Retraction Watch duplicate update records require event-level deduplication.
3. 2026 annual publication/retraction counts are incomplete and must not appear in full-year trends.
4. English-only guideline searches undercount global clinical contamination.
5. PMID-linked NIH analyses cannot stand in for global science.
6. Citation-context access is systematically better for open/full-text literature.
7. Retraction notices can be vague, legally constrained, or euphemistic.
8. Bulk publisher sweeps can create artificial retraction spikes.
9. Long correction latency causes severe right-censoring in recent cohorts.
10. Rare-event uncertainty should be shown on absolute counts as well as rates.

---

# Priority fixes before confirmatory run

1. Execute Pilot A and freeze denominator/event-join quality.
2. Freeze manual adjudication protocol.
3. Demonstrate detector coverage and calibration on Pilot B.
4. Require population-random audit information for Pilot C.
5. Validate semantic material-dependence classifier before global SCF.
6. Treat RLY as scenario/calibrated range until external effort data are assembled.
7. Keep IDY quasi-causal only if pre-trends/placebos pass.
8. Keep NWSB exploratory regardless of model performance in Paper 1.

---

# Recommendation

**GO.**

The project has enough conceptual novelty and empirical anchors to justify full pilot execution. The main scientific risk is not lack of ideas; it is overaggregation and overclaiming. The current architecture addresses that risk if its gates are enforced.
