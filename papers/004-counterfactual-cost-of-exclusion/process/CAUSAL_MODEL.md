# CAUSAL MODEL — ARIS4C004

Date: 2026-09-18

## Purpose

Prevent three distinct questions from being collapsed into one causal claim:

1. whether historically visible contributors with strong surviving mental-health evidence occupy different knowledge-network positions;
2. what happens to a knowledge network when a realized contributor's future participation is counterfactually reduced;
3. what portion of such a participation effect can be attributed to mental-health stigma/discrimination.

Only (2) is directly generated by the network simulator. (1) is descriptive under strong selection. (3) requires an additional exclusion mechanism and is usually scenario-based unless historically identified.

---

## Variables

- `MH` — underlying mental-health condition/history, partly latent historically.
- `E` — surviving evidence about MH.
- `D` — documentation/archival intensity.
- `S` — symptom/condition burden.
- `T` — treatment, access to treatment, and treatment burden.
- `X` — stigma/discrimination/formal institutional exclusion.
- `SES` — social class/resources/privilege.
- `GEO` — geography/language/institutional context.
- `F` — field/subfield and historical period.
- `A0` — baseline ability/training/opportunity before measured career entry.
- `P(t)` — productive participation over time.
- `N(t)` — observed network position over time.
- `Y(t)` — output/impact/knowledge propagation over time.
- `R(t)` — substitution/adaptation by collaborators, competitors, and outsiders.
- `V` — historical visibility/notability / probability of entering the candidate frame.

---

## DAG 1 — observed structural-position comparison

Conceptual arrows:

```text
A0, SES, GEO, F ──> P(t) ──> N(t) ──> Y(t) ──> V
        │             │        │        │       │
        └─────────────┴────────┴────────┘       │
                                                │
MH ──> S/T ──> P(t) ───────────────────────────┤
│                                              │
└──> X ──> P(t)                               │
                                               │
MH ───────────────> E <── D <── Y(t), V ◄──────┘
                          ▲
                          └── SES/GEO/F
```

Interpretation:

- `E` is not the same variable as `MH`.
- `E` becomes more observable when documentation is rich.
- `D` and `V` are affected by historical achievement/visibility as well as context.
- Restricting to notable/visible people (`V=1`) can create collider/selection associations.

### Identified quantity

A matched/weighted contrast in `N(t)` or CPE between evidence groups inside the visible-contributor frame is **descriptive of that frame**. It is not an estimate of `MH -> creativity`, `MH -> network centrality`, or population prevalence.

### Forbidden claim

> People with mental illness are inherently more important/creative/central.

The design cannot establish this.

## DAG 2 — participation intervention

```text
Observed history up to t0
      │
      ├── A0 / prior P / prior N / prior Y / field state
      │
      ▼
 do(P_i(t>=t0) = attenuated)
      │
      ├──────────────> focal future output
      │
      └──> collaborators / competitors / outsiders
                         │
                         ▼
                       R(t)
                         │
                         ▼
                 future N(t), Y(t), diversity,
                 entry, delay, recovery
```

### Estimand

`CPE_i(a,t0) = V(G_observed) - E[V(G_cf | do(P_i attenuated by a from t0), adaptation model)]`

This is a **model-based counterfactual participation effect** conditional on:

- the historically observed pre-`t0` network;
- the chosen intervention timing/intensity;
- the specified substitution/adaptation family;
- the measured outcome/value function.

It is not automatically an effect of mental-health discrimination.

### Key simulator rule

Substitute selection may use information available at or before the relevant simulation time, not future realized outcomes. Otherwise the counterfactual has look-ahead bias.

## DAG 3 — discrimination-scaled scenario

```text
MH / perceived MH / disclosure
            │
            ▼
      stigma/discrimination X
            │
            ▼
     participation P(t)
            │
            ▼
        CPE response
            │
            ▼
     network-level effect
```

Confounding/context:

```text
GEO, period, occupation, law, institution, SES
 ├──> X
 ├──> P(t)
 └──> network opportunity / Y(t)
```

### Identified quantity

If `X -> participation attenuation` is supplied only from modern stigma literature or a hypothetical range, the result is a **scenario analysis**:

`E[CPE(A)]` integrated over a stated attenuation distribution `A`.

It becomes a historical causal estimate only for cases where the exclusion event, its mental-health linkage, timing, and participation consequence are credibly documented or identified.

---

## Invisible-node problem

The candidate frame is assembled from people who left enough historical traces to be recognized as contributors. Severe exclusion may prevent a potential contributor from entering the observable knowledge system at all:

```text
potential contributor
      │
      ├── opportunity/inclusion ──> observable P/Y ──> V=1 ──> dataset
      │
      └── severe exclusion ───────> little/no observable trace ──> V=0
```

Therefore ARIS4C004 cannot estimate the total contribution of all people whom discrimination prevented from ever becoming visible.

### Claim limit

Preferred wording:

> Among historically realized and observable contributors, we estimate how network outcomes change under counterfactual participation attenuation. These effects capture an observable component of exclusion opportunity cost; they do not identify contributions of people erased before entering the historical record.

Do not call the resulting estimate "the total cost of mental-health discrimination."

---

## Selection and matching cautions

### Documentation intensity

`D` is useful for defining ascertainment/common support, but it may itself be downstream of fame/achievement. Treat it as an ascertainment variable, not a magic regression control.

Recommended uses:

- restrict comparisons to reasonable documentation common support;
- report exposure ascertainment by `D` strata;
- sensitivity analyses within high-documentation subsets;
- matching/weighting variants with and without `D`, interpreted carefully.

### Network position

Matching on baseline centrality answers a different question from not matching on it.

- **Contribution-context benchmark:** match era/field/output/documentation, not tight centrality. Asks whether focal people create different CPE distributions among broadly comparable contributors.
- **Role-matched benchmark:** additionally match centrality/brokerage. Asks whether any difference remains once the structural role is held approximately constant.

Neither is "the" uniquely correct control; report both because they target different contrasts.

## Estimand classification table

| Quantity | Type | Strongest defensible interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| MH evidence prevalence in frozen candidate frame | descriptive/ascertainment-dependent | prevalence of surviving evidence in this frame, not historical population prevalence |
| Network-position difference by MH evidence | descriptive matched contrast | association among visible contributors |
| `CPE_i(a,t0)` | model-based counterfactual | participation effect under stated adaptation assumptions |
| Mean CPE among Tier-A focal cases | model-based descriptive summary | distribution of modeled effects for evidence-supported realized contributors |
| Tier-A vs matched comparison CPE contrast | matched model-based contrast | whether focal realized contributors differ under the same intervention model |
| CPE integrated over hypothetical 25/50/75/100% attenuation | scenario/stress test | sensitivity of network outcomes to exclusion intensity |
| CPE integrated over modern stigma/discrimination estimates | modern-policy scenario | what such participation penalties could imply in the modeled network; not historical transport by default |
| CPE tied to a documented historical exclusion event | potentially historical causal/model-based | strongest case when timing/mechanism/link to MH are well established |
| Contribution of never-observed excluded people | not identified | must remain outside primary quantitative claims |

## Pre-analysis claim rules

1. Never use `MH -> genius` language.
2. Never infer `X` discrimination solely from MH evidence plus low productivity.
3. Never call unknown historical people "healthy controls."
4. Never interpret a fame-selected structural contrast as population prevalence or inherent group difference.
5. Report M0/M1/M2 differences because adaptation-model uncertainty is part of the result.
6. Permit and report negative CPE; outsider entry or alternative directions can improve selected outcomes.
7. Keep direct participation effect and discrimination attribution separate in figures/tables.
8. State the invisible-node limitation prominently in abstract/discussion if the project reaches manuscript stage.
