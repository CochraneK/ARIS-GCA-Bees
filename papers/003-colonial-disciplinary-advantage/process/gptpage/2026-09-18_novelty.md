## Bottom line

**Provisional verdict: novelty remains defensible, but only in a narrowed form.**

There is already meaningful quantitative literature showing that colonial ties shape specific modern research networks, funding patterns, and field-level knowledge-production geographies. Therefore ARIS4C003 must **not** claim novelty for the generic statement "colonial history still affects science."

The still-distinctive target is:

> a cross-national, cross-disciplinary test of whether historical imperial/colonial exposure predicts the *shape of national disciplinary specialization* as a function of an independently coded discipline-level imperial-entanglement gradient, triangulated across bibliometric specialization/impact, collaboration networks, and prestige/institutional outcomes.

No exact predecessor matching that full design was identified in this first targeted scan. A fuller systematic closest-prior-work search is still required before publication.

## Closest quantitative precedents identified

### 1. Raja et al. — palaeontology / knowledge-production geography

**Colonial history and global economics distort our understanding of deep-time biodiversity.** *Nature Ecology & Evolution* (2022). DOI: `10.1038/s41559-021-01608-8`.

- Unit: countries/research destinations and fossil-data production patterns.
- Exposure: colonial history plus socioeconomic factors.
- Outcome: distribution of fossil data / palaeontological knowledge production and "parachute science"-type imbalance.
- Overlap: strong on the basic historical-legacy→modern-knowledge-production logic.
- Difference: one field; not national cross-disciplinary specialization or an entanglement gradient.

### 2. Syed et al.-type infectious-disease collaboration precedent

A global analysis of swine infectious-disease research collaboration reported that country pairs were more likely to coauthor when they were in the same region, shared a language, or had a colonial relationship; the estimated colonial-relationship association was positive in their network model.

- Unit: country-pair collaboration network.
- Exposure: colonial relationship.
- Outcome: coauthorship.
- Overlap: supports H5 network persistence.
- Difference: domain-specific agricultural/veterinary infectious disease; does not test cross-disciplinary specialization.

### 3. UK infectious-disease research funding

**Infectious disease research investments follow colonial ties: questionable ethics.** *International Health* (2014). DOI: `10.1093/inthealth/iht036`.

- Unit: UK international infectious-disease research investments by recipient country.
- Exposure: historical links to UK/former British Empire/Commonwealth.
- Outcome: funding distribution.
- Main reported pattern: research investment was concentrated in countries with historical UK ties.
- Overlap: a direct modern resource-allocation legacy mechanism.
- Difference: single funder country, health domain, funding rather than disciplinary specialization.

### 4. International research collaboration in Africa

**International research collaboration: is Africa different? A cross-country panel data analysis.** *Scientometrics* (2023).

- Unit: cross-country African collaboration panel.
- Exposure: distance dimensions including colonial tie/common colonizer/common language.
- Outcome: international research collaboration.
- Relevant result: direct colonial ties and common language were associated with collaboration in the reported models, while sharing a former colonizer was not sufficient/robust in the same way.
- Overlap: strong dyadic/network precedent.
- Difference: Africa-focused; no cross-disciplinary historical-entanglement interaction.

### 5. Fisheries research in West Africa

**Historical ties shape fisheries research in West Africa.** *Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries* (2026).

- Unit: fisheries research relationships focused on West Africa.
- Exposure/mechanism: historical/colonial ties.
- Outcome: research contribution/collaboration geography.
- Overlap: recent field-specific evidence that former colonial powers retain disproportionate scientific ties/research focus.
- Difference: one research domain and one region.

### 6. Ghana qualitative collaboration dependency study

Nkansah, Oldac & Yang, **Dependency and neocolonialism in international research collaboration: evidence from a Ghanaian elite university.** *Higher Education* (2025). DOI: `10.1007/s10734-024-01393-w`.

- Unit: Ghanaian university researchers/administrators/policymakers.
- Method: qualitative interviews/case study.
- Overlap: mechanism evidence for persistent dependency in North–South collaboration.
- Difference: not cross-national bibliometric specialization.

## Scientific-specialization precedents

### Abramo et al. (2022)
**Revealing the scientific comparative advantage of nations: Common and distinctive features.** *Journal of Informetrics*, 16(1), 101244. DOI: `10.1016/j.joi.2021.101244`.

- 199 countries × 254 subject categories.
- Develops a country scientific-specialization/comparative-advantage profile.
- Key implication: ARIS4C003 does not need to invent the basic concept of national disciplinary comparative advantage; its contribution is explaining such patterns with historical exposure.

### Harzing & Giroud (2014)
**The competitive advantage of nations: An application to academia.** *Journal of Informetrics*, 8(1), 29–42. DOI: `10.1016/j.joi.2013.10.007`.

- Applies revealed comparative advantage logic to academic disciplines and national profiles.
- Key implication: use established specialization literature and avoid presenting RCA as a novel metric.

## Historical mechanism precedent: sociology

### Steinmetz (2013)
**A Child of the Empire: British Sociology and Colonialism, 1940s–1960s.** *Journal of the History of the Behavioral Sciences*. DOI: `10.1002/jhbs.21628`.

Documents that postwar British sociology was institutionally connected with colonial social-science research, colonial universities/research institutes, and training of colonial officials.

### Steinmetz (2026)
**Colonialism, Empire, and Sociology.** *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Sociology* (published 25 June 2026).

Important caution: sociology's relationship with empire is heterogeneous and historically contested. Entanglement must not be coded as ideological support for colonialism.

## Novelty claim that is NOT defensible

Do not write any of the following:

- "No one has studied colonial legacies in modern science."
- "This is the first study showing colonial ties influence research collaboration."
- "This is the first quantitative study of colonialism and knowledge production."
- "This is the first use of scientific comparative advantage across countries."

All are contradicted or made unsafe by identified prior work.

## Novelty claim potentially defensible after full review

> Prior studies have documented colonial persistence in specific scientific domains, funding patterns, and international collaborations, while a separate scientometric literature maps national disciplinary specialization. ARIS4C003 links these literatures by testing whether historical imperial/colonial exposure is associated with a systematic cross-disciplinary gradient in contemporary national specialization and influence, defined using discipline-level historical entanglement coded independently of present-day outcomes.

This wording still requires a fuller systematic search before manuscript use.

## Second targeted prior-work sweep — same date

A second search targeted older scientometric work and recent PPML/gravity collaboration studies. This materially sharpens the novelty boundary.

### 7. Nagtegaal & de Bruin (1994) — direct bibliometric neo-colonial network precedent

**The French connection and other neo-colonial patterns in the global network of science.** *Research Evaluation*, 4(2), 119–127. DOI: `10.1093/rev/4.2.119`.

- Quantified neo-colonial patterns in international scientific cooperation using bibliometrics.
- Proposed a country-level **Neo-Colonial Relations Index**.
- This is an important older predecessor and means ARIS4C003 cannot present the colonial-network idea as recent or newly quantified.
- It still differs from 003 because its target is dependency/network structure rather than a country × discipline specialization gradient moderated by a separately coded historical field-entanglement score.

### 8. Boshoff (2009) — Central African coauthorship dependency

**Neo-colonialism and research collaboration in Central Africa.** *Scientometrics*, 81(2), 413–434. DOI: `10.1007/s11192-008-2211-8`.

- Reported that 80% of Central African papers involved collaboration outside the region and 35% involved past colonial rulers.
- Strong precedent for the dyadic/network-persistence layer.
- Regional and collaboration-specific; it does not test cross-disciplinary comparative advantage.

### 9. Zhao (2026) — modern PPML/fractional-counting precedent

**Does distance influence international collaboration among scientists? Evidence from library and information science.** *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 13, 462. DOI: `10.1057/s41599-026-06767-2`.

- Uses a gravity-style PPML framework, high-dimensional fixed effects, and fractional counting.
- Treats shared language and colonial relationships as determinants of international collaboration.
- The fractional dyad rule assigns each multi-country paper total dyadic mass 1, which independently supports the normalization adopted in ARIS4C003.
- Field-specific (LIS) and does not estimate a colonial-tie × historical-discipline-entanglement gradient across many disciplines.

### 10. Scientific-discipline-space literature

Gandelman, Parcero & Roldán, **Opportunities to upgrade the scientific disciplines space** (2022), uses RCA-based discipline proximity across 174 countries and shows that scientific specialization can be treated as a structured cross-field space rather than independent subject silos.

This reinforces a useful framing for 003:

> the novelty is not the existence of disciplinary RCA, nor colonial persistence in coauthorship, but whether a historically defined *discipline characteristic* (IKES) systematically predicts how colonial exposure maps onto the shape of modern national scientific portfolios.

## Revised novelty hierarchy

**Already established in prior literature:**

1. countries have measurable scientific comparative-advantage/specialization profiles;
2. colonial and linguistic ties can persist in international coauthorship;
3. colonial ties can shape research funding and field-specific knowledge networks;
4. neo-colonial scientific-network patterns have been quantified since at least the 1990s.

**Still provisionally distinctive in ARIS4C003:**

1. an outcome-blind, independently coded **discipline-level imperial/colonial entanglement construct**;
2. a preregistered `historical exposure × discipline entanglement` interaction;
3. application across a broad fixed discipline set rather than one region/field;
4. triangulation across output specialization, normalized impact, dyadic collaboration, small-N imperial-center profiles, and later prestige layers;
5. explicit separation of former-colony, dyadic, and imperial-center estimands.

**Updated novelty risk:** moderate, not low. The project remains worth running, but the paper should lead with the cross-disciplinary historical-gradient design rather than with a broad claim that empire still shapes science.
