## Important construct rule

Scores concern evidence that imperial/colonial systems materially shaped a field's **institutions, methods, objects/data, training, research infrastructure, professional expansion, or postcolonial continuation**. Merely being useful to a colonial government is not sufficient for a high score.

## Evidence notes by field family

### Anthropology
Strong evidence supports deep colonial roots of ethnography, travel/field practice, population/cultural classification, and institutional knowledge production. Peter Pels' *Annual Review of Anthropology* article discusses colonial use and formation of ethnography; Oxford Bibliographies describes anthropology's historical origins as rooted in the colonial enterprise.

Key sources:
- Pels, P. (1997). *The Anthropology of Colonialism*. Annual Review of Anthropology 26:163–183. DOI `10.1146/annurev.anthro.26.1.163`.
- Kroll-Zeldin, O. (2016). *Colonialism*. Oxford Bibliographies in Anthropology.

### Archaeology
Modern archaeology developed in national/imperial contexts, and archaeological practice, collecting, museums, and interpretation were used in formal/informal imperial projects. Recent Oxford histories explicitly discuss archaeology as a tool of imperialism and the colonial contexts in which archaeological theories/practices emerged.

Key sources:
- Díaz-Andreu, M. (2024). *Archaeology, Nationalism, Imperialism, Colonialism, and the Postcolonial Turn*. Oxford Handbook of the History of Archaeology.
- *Colonial and Post-colonial Archaeologies* (2014), Oxford Handbook of Archaeological Theory.

### Geography
Colonial exploration, survey, mapping, territorial knowledge, and resource description were deeply intertwined with geography. Historical social-science work identifies geography as integral to exploration preceding conquest and to construction/development of colonial territory.

Key sources:
- Steinmetz, G. (2023). *The Earliest Colonial Social Sciences and Their Engagement with Sociology: Geography, Law, Economics, and the Sciences of the Psyche*. In *The Colonial Origins of Modern Social Thought*.
- Jones & Phillips (2005). *Unsettling Geographical Horizons: Exploring Premodern and Non-European Imperialism*. Annals of the AAG.

### Development Studies
The academic field emerged in the post-WWII development/modernization era immediately following formal colonial rule and has a substantial literature on colonial continuity, "imperial amnesia," aid, modernization, and postcolonial development administration.

Key source:
- Kapoor, I. (2023). *Decolonising Development Studies*. Review of International Studies.

### Linguistics
Missionary-colonial settings generated large bodies of grammars, dictionaries, descriptions, and typological knowledge; missionary grammars are explicitly treated as part of the history of linguistics and required adaptation of European grammatical models to new languages.

Key source:
- Zwartjes, O. (2018). *Missionary Grammars*. Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Linguistics.

### Tropical Medicine / Public Health
This is among the clearest institutional links. Tropical medicine grew from colonial disease problems and was supported by colonial states/commercial networks; colonial health surveillance and specialist schools became durable infrastructure.

Key sources:
- Bhattacharya, N. (2012). *Contagion and Enclaves: Tropical Medicine in Colonial India*.
- Bump & Aniebo (2022). *Colonialism, malaria, and the decolonization of global health*.
- Manderson, L. (1999). *Public health developments in colonial Malaya*. American Journal of Public Health.

### Agriculture & Forestry
Botanical gardens, crop transfer, plantation systems, agricultural stations, forestry, plant pathology and economic botany were tightly linked to imperial expansion and commercial extraction. The Cambridge History of Science documents science-led colonial development in agriculture; Oxford history of botanic gardens links them directly to colonial expansion and cash-crop systems.

Key sources:
- Chakrabarti & Worboys (2020). *Science and Imperialism since 1870*. Cambridge History of Science.
- *Lay Expertise, Botanical Science, and Botanic Gardens as “Contact Zones”* (2021). Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Environmental Science.

### Geology / Earth-resource sciences
Geological survey, mapping, minerals and resource prospecting had direct imperial utility and created professional posts, surveys, museums and specimen flows. Historical work describes empire as a major hinterland for British geology and links geology/mineralogy to imperial competition and territorial/resource claims.

Key sources:
- Stafford, R. (2026). *Annexing the landscapes of the past: British imperial geology in the nineteenth century*.
- Wu, S. (2022). *Geology, Mineralogy, and Exploration*. Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Asian History.

### Sociology
Evidence is direct rather than merely thematic. British postwar sociology was institutionally connected to the Colonial Social Science Research Council, colonial universities/research institutes, and LSE training of colonial officials. French sociology likewise had a large colonial research grouping.

Key sources:
- Steinmetz, G. (2013). *A Child of the Empire: British Sociology and Colonialism, 1940s–1960s*. DOI `10.1002/jhbs.21628`.
- Steinmetz, G. (2023). *The Colonial Origins of Modern Social Thought*.
- Steinmetz, G. (2026). *Colonialism, Empire, and Sociology*. Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Sociology.

### Political Science / International Relations
Colonial administration was treated historically as a branch/problem of political science; critical disciplinary histories identify imperial infrastructure and racial/colonial ordering in the formation of political science/IR. Because this link varies by subfield and national tradition, scores are more conservative than for anthropology or colonial sociology.

Key sources:
- Ireland, A. (1907). *On the Need for a Scientific Study of Colonial Administration*. Proceedings of the American Political Science Association.
- Kristensen, P. M. (2022). *Subject matters: Imperialism and the constitution of International Relations*. Review of International Studies.

### Law
Empire created extensive legal transplantation, pluralism, comparative-law problems and colonial legal institutions. British comparative law in the nineteenth century focused substantially on imperial problems and laws of dominions.

Key sources:
- Cairns, J. W. (2019). *Development of Comparative Law in Great Britain*. Oxford Handbook of Comparative Law.
- Burset, C. R. (2023). *An Empire of Laws: Legal Pluralism in British Colonial Policy*.

### Economics
Colonial political economy, resource extraction and developmentalism were important sites of economic argument and expertise. Steinmetz's disciplinary history documents economists' involvement in twentieth-century colonial developmentalist agendas. Scores remain moderate because economics also had major noncolonial foundations.

Key source:
- Steinmetz, G. (2023), chapter on geography, law, economics and colonial social sciences.

### Public Administration / Social Policy
This field has unusually direct evidence: American public-administration pioneers participated in post-1898 colonial administration and studied European empires; colonial administrative models were transferred to colonies and influenced postcolonial administrative systems and development administration.

Key sources:
- Roberts, A. (2020). *Bearing the White Man’s Burden: American Empire and the Origin of Public Administration*. DOI `10.1093/ppmgov/gvz018`.
- Peters, B. G. (2021). *Transferring Traditions: The Colonial Experience*.

### Education
Colonial and missionary systems transplanted curricula, language, teacher training, universities and educational administration; postcolonial systems often retained institutional continuities. The score is moderate rather than high because evidence of colonial schooling is not automatically evidence that the **academic discipline of Education** was founded through colonial research.

Key sources:
- Madeira & Correia (2019). *Colonial Education and Anticolonial Struggles*. Oxford Handbook of the History of Education.
- Kallaway (2019). *National Education Systems: Africa*. Oxford Handbook of the History of Education.

### History
Colonial archives and imperial history were important, but historical evidence reviewed here indicates colonial specialists were relatively slow to be integrated into professional history compared with statistics/demography/anthropology. Hence a conservative global score despite strong imperial historiographies.

Key source:
- Steinmetz, G. (2023). *Other Neighboring Social Sciences... History, Statistics, Demography, and Anthropology*.

### Demography / Population Studies
Census, population enumeration, classification and statistical administration were core colonial governance technologies. Historical work describes statistics and demography as closely entwined with colonial social science and administration.

Key source:
- Steinmetz, G. (2023). *Other Neighboring Social Sciences... History, Statistics, Demography, and Anthropology*.

### Mathematics
Colonial school systems transmitted European mathematics curricula and teacher-training structures, but current evidence is much stronger for **mathematics education** than for empire as a foundational driver of mathematical research as a discipline. Therefore low primary scores with D8/D10/D11 acknowledging educational transmission.

Key source:
- Schubring, G. (2021). *On processes of coloniality and decoloniality of knowledge: notions for analysing the international history of mathematics teaching*. ZDM Mathematics Education.

### Physics
Imperial science created expeditionary, metrological, astronomical and institutional opportunities, but the reviewed general histories emphasize particularly strong colonial integration in biological/environmental/applied sciences rather than physics as a whole. Provisional score therefore remains low; targeted second-coder evidence search is required.

Key source:
- Chakrabarti & Worboys (2020). *Science and Imperialism since 1870*. Cambridge History of Science.

### Chemistry
Chemistry cannot be treated as a clean zero-entanglement control. Imperial laboratories analyzed minerals/natural products; the British Imperial Institute functioned as an applied research laboratory, and colonies developed applied chemistry tied to resources and agriculture. The academic field nevertheless had broad nonimperial foundations, so the provisional score is moderate.

Key source:
- Chakrabarti & Worboys (2020). *Science and Imperialism since 1870*. Cambridge History of Science.

### Computer Science
Computer science arose much later than the main era of formal overseas empire. Coloniality is important in contemporary HCI4D/postcolonial-computing critiques, but that is different from empire being foundational to the discipline's original institutional formation. Hence a low historical IKES with some postwar-development continuity.

Key source:
- Nothias, T. (2025). *An intellectual history of digital colonialism*. Journal of Communication.

### Materials Science
The frozen comparator is operationalized here as **Materials Science**, not civil/military engineering broadly, because engineering infrastructure was itself deeply imperial. Resource/mineral supply and applied imperial laboratories justify nonzero scores, but the modern discipline institutionalized largely later and through broader industrial/scientific pathways.

Key source:
- Chakrabarti & Worboys (2020). *Science and Imperialism since 1870*.

## First-coder lessons that change the original intuition

1. **Anthropology/Sociology are not the only plausible high-entanglement fields.** Agriculture/Forestry, Tropical Medicine, Geography, Geology, Development Studies and Public Administration show strong historical mechanisms.
2. **Chemistry is not a clean negative control.** Imperial applied chemistry/resource networks make it a useful middle case.
3. **A simple social-science-vs-STEM contrast would be wrong.** Several natural/applied sciences were strongly imperial while Mathematics/Computer Science appear much less directly entangled under this construct.
4. **“Colonial use” must not be confused with “discipline formation.”** Education and Mathematics illustrate why the evidence bar should focus on academic/professional institutionalization, not merely colonial teaching/practice.
5. The final confirmatory test should use the continuous IKES rather than hand-labeling only Anthropology/Sociology as treated disciplines.
