# LITERATURE SEED — ARIS4C003

This is a **verified starting set**, not a completed systematic review. Entries were checked against publisher/documentation pages during ARIS entry on 2026-09-18.

## A. Colonial history and knowledge production

### Raja et al. (2022)
**Colonial history and global economics distort our understanding of deep-time biodiversity.** *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

DOI: `10.1038/s41559-021-01608-8`

Why it matters: a direct methodological precedent showing that colonial legacy and contemporary socioeconomic factors can be modeled against the geography of present-day scientific knowledge production. It is field-specific (palaeontology), so it does not answer ARIS4C003's cross-disciplinary specialization question.

## B. Scientific specialization / comparative advantage

### Abramo et al. (2022)
**Revealing the scientific comparative advantage of nations: Common and distinctive features.** *Journal of Informetrics*, 16(1), 101244.

DOI: `10.1016/j.joi.2021.101244`

Why it matters: maps scientific specialization for 199 countries across 254 subject categories and develops an output/impact-based approach to national scientific comparative advantage.

### Harzing & Giroud (2014)
**The competitive advantage of nations: An application to academia.** *Journal of Informetrics*, 8(1), 29–42.

DOI: `10.1016/j.joi.2013.10.007`

Why it matters: applies revealed comparative advantage/competitive-advantage thinking to academic disciplinary profiles and includes social sciences.

### Relevant specialization literature
Search and review the literature on relative specialization index (RSI), revealed comparative advantage (RCA), citation-based RCA, and alternative field-normalized specialization measures before freezing the primary outcome. Classic RCA is useful descriptively but should not be treated as a literal structural parameter.

## C. Sociology and empire

### Steinmetz (2013)
**A Child of the Empire: British Sociology and Colonialism, 1940s–1960s.** *Journal of the History of the Behavioral Sciences*, 49(4), 353–378.

DOI: `10.1002/jhbs.21628`

Why it matters: documents the institutional entanglement of postwar British sociology with colonial social-science funding, colonial universities/research institutes, and training of colonial officials. This supports a plausible discipline-specific mechanism rather than merely a thematic association.

### Steinmetz (2026)
**Colonialism, Empire, and Sociology.** *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Sociology*.

Published 25 June 2026.

Why it matters: current overview of sociology's long relationship with colonialism and empire across national traditions; useful for avoiding an oversimplified story in which sociology was uniformly pro-colonial or mechanically created by empire.

## D. Data/method documentation

### ICOW Colonial History Data
Paul R. Hensel, Issue Correlates of War Project.

Current public page describes colonial/dependency histories for states in the COW interstate system, including colonial ruler and independence information. The site asks researchers not to redistribute the dataset and instead to direct users to the official source. Therefore ARIS4C003 should commit reconstruction code/instructions, not republish ICOW raw files.

Official source: `https://www.paulhensel.org/icowcol.html`

### Leiden Ranking Open Edition 2025
The 2025 Open Edition is based on OpenAlex and exposes separate scientific-impact and collaboration indicators. Documented indicators include P/PP(top 1/5/10%), normalized citation measures, international collaboration, and fractional/full-counting options. Trend windows extend back to 2006–2009 and through 2020–2023.

Official indicator documentation: `https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators`

### THE Subject Rankings 2026
THE uses 18 indicators grouped under Teaching, Research Environment, Research Quality, International Outlook, and Industry, with subject-specific weights. It publishes 11 broad subject rankings covering narrower disciplines. Treat as a composite institutional layer rather than a pure research-output measure.

Official methodology: `https://www.timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/world-university-rankings-subject-2026-methodology`

## E. Highest-priority missing literature searches

1. Quantitative studies of former colonial ties and modern scientific coauthorship/collaboration.
2. Cross-national bibliometric studies where colonial history predicts field specialization, not merely overall output.
3. Histories of imperial geography, archaeology, linguistics, tropical medicine, geology/mining, agriculture/forestry, development studies, demography/census, law and education.
4. Studies of Anglophone/database-indexing bias by discipline and country.
5. Literature on historical path dependence in national scientific specialization.
6. Work on migration/shocks to scientific human capital (e.g., forced migration of scientists) as competing or complementary historical-knowledge-capital mechanisms.
7. Ranking-methodology research that separates reputational persistence from bibliometric performance.

## F. Novelty discipline

Until the searches above are completed, do not claim that no prior paper has studied colonial legacy and disciplinary advantage. The provisional novelty target is specifically:

> a cross-national, cross-disciplinary test in which historical imperial/colonial exposure is interacted with an independently coded discipline-level imperial-entanglement construct and evaluated against multiple contemporary outcome families (specialization, impact, network, prestige).
