# ARIS4C006 · Pivot assessment: country-level alphabetical burden

Last updated: 2026-09-18

## Trigger

The first real-data pilot showed that the Chinese surname-initial distribution is strongly shifted toward particular middle/late Latin initials:

- population-weighted mean initial rank: **16.21** (uniform A–Z benchmark = 13.5);
- L/Z/W alone: **46.22%** of the ChineseNames population baseline;
- U–Z: **40.04%**.

This suggested a potentially sharper hypothesis:

> When international teams alphabetize authors, do China-affiliated scholars systematically appear later because the Chinese surname distribution is shifted later than surname distributions in many collaborator countries?

## Immediate novelty search result

### Direct collision found

Charles Crabtree, John B. Holbein, and Kiyoteru Tsutsui currently list a manuscript:

**“The Tyranny of Alphabetical Ordering: The Uneven Distribution of Authorship Credits by Authors' Country of Origin”**

Status on the authors' public pages as of 2026-09-18: **revised and resubmitted at the Journal of Informetrics**.

The public abstract states that the paper:

- studies a century of leading-journal publications in economics, political science, and sociology;
- combines publication patterns with nearly half a billion name profiles across >100 countries;
- estimates country-level surname-initial distributions;
- finds that countries whose surnames cluster later alphabetically appear later in author bylines;
- frames this as a structural global inequality in academic credit, with disadvantages concentrated in South America and East/Central Asia.

## Decision

**Do not pivot ARIS4C006 into a generic country-of-origin / international-team alphabetical-disadvantage paper.**

That contribution is now directly occupied by current work likely to publish soon. A China-only demonstration of the same country-distribution mechanism would be narrower rather than more novel.

## What the collision teaches us

The pilot's surname-distribution finding remains valuable, but as:

1. a motivation for why China is an unusually informative setting;
2. a population calibration / measurement check;
3. an external-context result to compare with the Crabtree–Holbein–Tsutsui framework;
4. a reason to emphasize **within-China / within-author longitudinal institutional exposure** rather than country rankings.

The research should cite this R&R prominently if/when publicly citable details remain available and re-check its publication status immediately before manuscript submission.

## Residual ARIS4C006 space

The paper remains distinguishable if it focuses on questions not answered by the country-level R&R:

### A. Continuous local exposure

Rather than country surname distributions alone, estimate each scholar's lagged exposure to journal/field/year contexts that actually use alphabetical author ordering beyond chance.

### B. Within-author exposure changes

Use authors who move across publication environments with different alphabetization norms. Scholar fixed effects absorb stable person-level traits; identification comes from how the same stable surname interacts with changing institutional exposure.

### C. Mechanism-proximal behavior

Study whether later-surname China-based scholars:

- select different collaborators;
- avoid larger teams in high-alphabetization contexts;
- deviate from alphabetical ordering strategically;
- alter journal/field choices;
- experience different listed-position visibility conditional on local norms.

### D. Longitudinal downstream consequences

Only after A–C are validated, test a small number of career outcomes. This differs from a cross-country surname-composition comparison.

### E. Chinese-specific measurement contribution

A validated pipeline for Chinese-character/Romanized surname parsing plus explicit OpenAlex identity-error audits can itself be methodologically useful, particularly because Chinese name ambiguity is severe and may contaminate bibliometric inequality estimates.

## Potential extension, not primary novelty claim

A preregistered subgroup analysis can still compare all-China vs internationally mixed teams **within the China-based author panel** to test whether internationalization changes exposure to alphabetical norms.

But the claim must be:

> international collaboration modifies an individual Chinese scholar's institutional exposure history

rather than:

> China is structurally disadvantaged because its surnames are later alphabetically.

The latter overlaps directly with the active country-of-origin manuscript.

## Novelty verdict after collision

**NARROW / GO.**

This collision does not kill ARIS4C006. It removes a tempting but occupied pivot and makes the intended contribution more precise:

> **individual longitudinal exposure to author-order institutions inside the China-based scholarly system, with mechanism-first outcomes and unusually rigorous name/identity measurement.**
