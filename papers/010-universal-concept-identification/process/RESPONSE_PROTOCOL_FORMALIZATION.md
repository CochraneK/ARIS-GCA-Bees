# ARIS4C010 · Response-Protocol Formalization

## Protocol alphabets

Let:

- (R_2 = \{Y,N\})
- (R_3 = \{Y,N,M\}), where (M) = MAYBE
- (R_6 = \{Y,N,B,U,D,X\})

with:

- (B) = BORDERLINE
- (U) = UNKNOWN
- (D) = UNDEFINED
- (X) = BOTH

## Deterministic P6 → P3 coarsening

Define:

[
g(r)=
\begin{cases}
Y & r=Y \\
N & r=N \\
M & r\in\{B,U,D,X\}
\end{cases}
]

Thus P3 is a natural **coarsening** of P6 at the label level.

This yields two distinct empirical questions.

### Q1 · refinement content

Among judgments that P3 would treat as MAYBE, does P6 partition responses into stable and theoretically interpretable subclasses?

### Q2 · protocol reactivity

Does asking people to make the finer distinction change their coarse judgment itself?

For each target-query pair, compare:

1. the observed P3 distribution over ({Y,N,M});
2. the observed P6 distribution after applying (g).

If these differ substantially, the response protocol is not merely changing output resolution; it is changing the judgment process.

## Data-processing baseline

If P3 responses were literally generated by applying (g) to the same latent P6 response, then the data-processing inequality implies that deterministic coarsening cannot increase mutual information about the target:

[
I(C;g(R_6)) \le I(C;R_6).
]

But the human experiment does **not** assume that observed P3 is generated this way. Different instructions and alphabets can alter decisions, response time, confidence, and category boundaries.

Therefore the information-theoretic ordering is a theoretical baseline, not an empirical conclusion.

## Why raw question count is not enough

Maximum response-alphabet capacities differ:

- P2: (log_2 2 = 1) bit/response;
- P3: (log_2 3 \approx 1.585) bits/response;
- P6: (log_2 6 \approx 2.585) bits/response.

A six-way response can therefore reduce decision-tree depth for purely mechanical reasons even if the semantic distinctions are not psychologically reliable.

The confirmatory analysis must report alongside question count:

- response time;
- empirical response entropy;
- target information per response;
- target information per second/cost;
- retest reliability;
- pairwise human agreement;
- P3 vs coarsened-P6 distribution divergence.

## Proposed protocol metrics

### 1. Coarsening divergence

For each sufficiently rated pair, compare actual P3 (P_3(r|c,q)) with coarsened P6 (g(P_6)).

Report Jensen–Shannon divergence and total-variation distance.

Low divergence means P6 behaves approximately as a refinement of P3.
High divergence indicates protocol-induced judgment reactivity.

### 2. Fine-state utilization

Conditional on P6 returning a nonbinary state:

[
P(B,U,D,X \mid R_6\notin\{Y,N\}).
]

If nearly all mass collapses into one subtype, six labels may be unnecessary.

### 3. Fine-state reliability

Compute retest agreement and inter-rater concentration separately for B/U/D/X.

A category that is frequently used but unreliably distinguished does not automatically improve the benchmark.

### 4. Fine-graining query gain

Let (L^*_{P3}) and (L^*_{P6}) be optimal semantic-policy costs under calibrated response models.

[
G_{fine}=L^*_{P3}-L^*_{P6}.
]

Positive values mean P6 reduces query cost. This must be interpreted jointly with extra response time and cognitive cost.

### 5. Time-normalized gain

A practical efficiency form is:

[
E_{time}=\frac{I(C;R)}{E[T_R]}
]

or an equivalent cost model frozen before confirmatory testing.

## P2 cannot be obtained by a neutral deterministic coarsening of P3

Mapping MAYBE to YES or NO requires an additional forced-choice rule. Different rules produce different P2 behavior.

Therefore P2 is best treated as an intervention that **removes abstention/escape**, not as a simple lower-resolution encoding of P3.

## Confirmatory interpretation

Evidence for the distinctive P6 mechanism requires more than:

> P6 has fewer questions than P2.

The stronger pattern is:

1. P3 improves over P2 where binary forcing is inappropriate;
2. P6 response subtypes are used in semantically structured ways;
3. P6 retains acceptable reliability;
4. P6 improves downstream identification or calibration relative to P3;
5. improvement is not explained only by larger response alphabet or longer response time.
