# ARIS4C016 · The Global Grammar of Swearing

**Question:** what does humanity repeatedly choose to turn into taboo language, and how much of that structure is universal, inherited through language history, or associated with culture?

This project is **not** a multilingual swear-word list. It treats taboo language as an observable window into semantic taboo, social norms, pragmatic aggression, sound symbolism and cultural transmission.

## Current scope

Phase 0 starts with the open Sulpizio et al. (2024) multi-lab dataset:
- 13 languages;
- 17 countries;
- 1,046 participants in free generation;
- speaker-centered ratings and manually coded taboo categories;
- public materials, data and analysis scripts.

The project will first reproduce and extend that dataset before claiming a global atlas.

## Core outputs

1. **Taboo Fingerprint** — a community-level profile of semantic sources, pragmatic functions and intensity.
2. **Language vs culture decomposition** — hierarchical models separating language, country/community and language-family variation.
3. **Phylogenetically corrected similarity analysis** — test whether taboo similarity tracks linguistic genealogy, geography or cultural distance.
4. **Phonological universals test** — preregistered replication/extension of candidate sound-symbolic patterns.
5. **Global Swearing Atlas** — only after empirical coverage is broad enough to justify the label.

## Guardrails

- no explicit slur catalogue is required for public-facing outputs;
- translations are not treated as semantic identity;
- country-level values are not interpreted as individual psychology;
- language-family relatedness is modeled rather than ignored;
- social-media frequency is secondary evidence, not a substitute for speaker elicitation;
- exploratory findings cannot be promoted to universals without held-out language/community replication.
