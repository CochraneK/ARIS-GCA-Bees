# ARIS4C016 Prior-Art Matrix

Updated: 2026-09-18

## Core prior art

### Sulpizio et al. (2024) — Taboo language across the globe
Behavior Research Methods 56, 3794–3813. DOI: 10.3758/s13428-024-02376-6.

What it already establishes:
- bottom-up taboo-word generation;
- 13 languages and 17 countries across inhabited continents;
- 1,046 participants in Study 1;
- semantic/affective ratings;
- substantial cross-country variability;
- open materials, data and scripts.

Implication for ARIS4C016:
A project that merely collects taboo words from multiple languages is not novel enough. Phase 0 must reproduce this resource and extend the explanatory design.

### Lev-Ari & McKay (2023) — The sound of swearing
Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 30, 1103–1114. DOI: 10.3758/s13423-022-02202-0.

What it already establishes:
- systematic cross-linguistic candidate phonetic regularity;
- approximants were underrepresented in an exploratory swear-word comparison;
- speakers from several language groups judged approximant-containing pseudowords less swear-like;
- English minced oaths contained more approximants than source swear words.

Implication:
"Swear words sound harsh" is not novel. ARIS4C016 must test preregistered phonological claims under stronger lexical, genealogical and held-out-language controls.

### Ljung (2011) — Swearing: A Cross-Cultural Linguistic Study
Palgrave Macmillan.

What it already contributes:
- definition and typology of swearing;
- comparison of English with roughly two dozen other languages;
- historical and grammatical/pragmatic treatment.

Implication:
A descriptive typology alone is insufficient novelty.

## Novelty claim to test, not assume

ARIS4C016 aims to integrate four layers that are usually separated:
1. bottom-up community taboo inventories;
2. semantic/pragmatic "taboo fingerprints";
3. language genealogy and phonological structure;
4. explicitly aligned sociocultural predictors.

The strongest candidate contribution is a variance-decomposition and predictive framework asking how much taboo-language structure is attributable to lexical/speaker, community, language and genealogical levels, followed by held-out-family tests.

## Falsifiers of novelty

The novelty claim should be downgraded if prior literature is found that already:
- performs a comparable hierarchical language-vs-culture variance decomposition on broad taboo inventories;
- uses phylogenetic comparative models on taboo semantic profiles;
- provides a broader open multilingual elicitation dataset with equivalent ratings and community metadata;
- demonstrates the same proposed cultural predictors with held-out cross-language replication.

## Search gaps still open

- non-English scholarship on profanity typology;
- historical corpora for taboo-domain shift;
- sign-language taboo systems;
- code-switching and multilingual profanity;
- child acquisition and age grading;
- register-specific taboo systems;
- censorship-driven euphemism evolution.

These are expansion tracks, not excuses to inflate the Phase-0 scope.
