# ARIS4C016 Paper Architecture

Updated: 2026-09-18

## Main-paper question

**What parts of taboo-language structure are stable across communities, and
what parts remain community-sensitive once measurement non-equivalence,
lexical identity and language genealogy are made explicit?**

This is narrower and more defensible than trying to solve every question about
swearing in one paper.

## Study 1 — Measurement audit

Data:
Sulpizio et al. (2024) Study 1.

Primary results already established:
- large cross-sample differences in elicitation yield;
- heterogeneous/missing category annotation;
- flat source labels mix semantic, pragmatic and social-indexical levels;
- naïve semantic fingerprints are not comparable.

Contribution:
a reproducible audit showing why cross-cultural lexical prevalence cannot be
interpreted without harmonization and annotation-coverage reporting.

## Study 2 — Ontology and reliability

Data:
deterministic ~300-row multilingual audit sample.

Design:
- independent Coder A/B;
- orthogonal multi-axis ontology;
- preserve unresolved/context-required labels;
- native-language review where needed;
- agreement and adjudication versioning.

Contribution:
a validated measurement layer rather than a translated swear-word dictionary.

## Study 3 — Stable core vs community modulation

Data:
Sulpizio et al. Study 2 repeated-language items.

Primary English design:
- 139 lexical items shared by at least two English communities;
- item-fixed-effects community model.

Current Phase-0 result:
- tabooness community partial R² after item FE ≈ .027;
- offensiveness ≈ .036;
- lexical identity dominates, with modest but measurable community modulation.

Contribution:
directly quantify invariance/context sensitivity without comparing unrelated
words across languages.

## Study 4 — Measurement-corrected Taboo Fingerprints

Requires Study-2 ontology reliability gate to pass.

Outputs:
- semantic-source fingerprint;
- target fingerprint;
- social-indexical fingerprint;
- pragmatic-function fingerprint where context permits;
- type-weighted and production-weighted versions;
- unresolved/missingness bounds.

No country ranking is allowed when uncertainty is dominated by missing or
unresolved coding.

## Study 5 — Genealogy-aware cross-language tests

Current Phase-0 structure:
- 18 community samples;
- 13 languages;
- 5 top-level Glottolog families;
- 8/13 languages Indo-European.

Use:
- language-level partial pooling;
- genealogy-aware sensitivity;
- held-out family stress tests.

Strong universality claims are deferred until family diversity expands.

## Validation / companion track — phonology

Replicate the prior approximant hypothesis with a standardized G2P/IPA layer
and matched neutral controls.

This can be:
- a validation section if coverage is strong; or
- a companion paper if pronunciation/control construction becomes a large
  independent project.

## Public artifact — Global Swearing Atlas

The atlas is an output layer, not the primary evidence.

It should show:
- what is observed;
- annotation coverage;
- uncertainty;
- what is unresolved;
- language/community/sample provenance.

It should not display apparent precision that the data do not support.

## Claims ladder

### Allowed now
- existing annotation protocols differ substantially across samples;
- same-item taboo/offensiveness ratings show high stability plus modest
  English community modulation;
- the current dataset is genealogically/factorially imbalanced;
- a flat category taxonomy cannot directly recover a comparable semantic
  fingerprint.

### Requires dual-coder reliability
- community semantic-domain profiles;
- domain-specific cross-community contrasts.

### Requires expanded language/family coverage
- cross-linguistic universals;
- language-family effects;
- broad culture-vs-language decomposition.

### Requires stronger identification
- causal claims about religion, kinship, collectivism, censorship or other
  cultural variables.

## Manuscript strategy

The paper should lead with the surprising methodological result:

> A global taboo dataset can look cross-cultural while still encoding
> laboratory-specific annotation practices into the outcome.

Then show that once lexical identity is held fixed, a stable core plus modest
community modulation emerges.

This creates a coherent arc:
**measurement problem → repair → stronger comparison → atlas.**
