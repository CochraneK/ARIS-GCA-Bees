# Prior-art matrix · ARIS4C013

This matrix separates four distinct literatures that are often conflated when people ask whether birth timing is related to death timing.

| Study | Population / N | Exposure / timing question | Main result | What it supports | Main caution for ARIS4C013 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alderson 1975, JECH | England & Wales deaths age 75+ | month of death relative to month of birth | small excess in birth month and following ~3 months; about 1% of annual deaths | H2-type anniversary timing candidate | month-level only; author noted possible birth-month recording error |
| Phillips, Van Voorhees & Ruth 1992, Psychosomatic Medicine | two California adult mortality samples, total N≈2.75M | weekly death timing around birthday | female post-birthday peak; male pre-birthday pattern | H2 anniversary / psychosocial hypothesis | effect size small; subgroup pattern complex; needs independent replication |
| Phillips, Ruth & Wagner 1993, Lancet | 28,169 Chinese-American deaths + 412,632 matched white controls | Chinese birth-year element × disease combinations | reported 1.3–4.9 years shorter survival for selected “ill-fated” combinations | historical H4-adjacent claim | disease mappings/exclusions flexible; not a direct birth-date→death-date test |
| Doblhammer 1999; Doblhammer & Vaupel 2001 | Austria, Denmark, Australia; >1M in later work | month of birth and adult longevity | seasonal birth effects; Northern/Southern Hemisphere phase shift | H1 developmental/seasonal environment | not an anniversary effect and not astrology |
| Verhulst 2000, Medical Hypotheses | anthroposophic pioneers | lunar phase at birth and death | group distributions correlated, but not at individual level | example of astronomical-time hypothesis | highly selected sample; individual coupling not supported |
| Young & Hade 2004, JAMA | U.S. cancer deaths | death before/after birthday and holidays | no convincing birthday postponement in overall sample | boundary / null evidence for H2 | cancer-specific; tests postponement more than same-day excess |
| Smith 2006, Health Psychology | California mortality, original + independent periods | Five Elements × disease | more complete analysis and independent periods failed to replicate 1993 result | strong falsifier for flexible H4 claims | demonstrates mapping and selection sensitivity |
| Austin et al. 2006, J Clin Epidemiol | 10,674,945 Ontario residents; 223 diagnoses | zodiac signs and health outcomes | discovery “associations” arose under massive multiple testing and failed validation | metascience warning | requires holdout + multiplicity control + matched pseudo-calendars |
| Ajdacic-Gross et al. 2012, Ann Epidemiol | Swiss deaths 1969–2008 | exact birthday mortality | reported 13.8% overall excess on birthdays | H2 exact-date candidate | cause/sex heterogeneity; must control calendar marginals and date quality |
| Ueda et al. 2013, PLOS ONE | Sweden, N=6,194,745 | month of birth and mortality | higher later-life mortality for spring/summer births; lowest around November | H1 seasonal birth effect | effect is about long-run mortality, not birth–death phase coupling |
| Ueda et al. 2014 | Sweden, N=4,240,338 | month of birth × cause-specific mortality | associations for cardiovascular/infectious mortality, not tumors/external causes | H1 mechanism heterogeneity | cause-specific pattern argues against one universal timing law |
| Neugart et al. / Germany cancer study 2014 | 3,257,520 cancer deaths, 1995–2009 | week before/after birthday, Christmas, Easter | no convincing birthday postponement effect | boundary/null evidence for H2 | cancer-only; event-window design |
| Japan milestone-birthday suicide study 2019 | Japanese suicide deaths | birthday and milestone birthday timing | elevated suicide risk on some birthday categories | H2 behavioral/psychological pathway | outcome-specific and culturally mediated |
| Japanese older-adult month-of-birth study 2016 | JAGES older adults | month of birth and mortality | month-of-birth differences; authors discuss historical registration practices | H1 plus administrative/cultural mechanisms | registration artifacts are a plausible causal competitor |
| Weitkunat et al. 2025, F1000Research | Swiss adults, 1987–2022, N≈1.70M | exact birth–death day distance | ~8% birthday excess after season/day-of-week modeling | modern H2 replication candidate | excludes imputed dates; supports importance of precision metadata |

## Synthesis

### What is already well established enough to be a baseline
- Birth season/month can be associated with later mortality in some populations.
- Mortality itself is strongly seasonal.
- Exact-date administrative data can contain nonrandom missingness, imputation, and date heaping.
- Flexible astrology/zodiac screens can generate convincing-looking false positives without independent validation.

### What remains genuinely testable
- Whether an exact-day birthday/anniversary effect reproduces in a large U.S. administrative source after explicit date-quality controls.
- Whether any effect is stable across years, sex, age, and data-quality strata rather than driven by administrative coding.
- Whether cultural-calendar representations add prediction beyond ordinary seasonality and birthday distance.
- Whether prespecified traditional Chinese date encodings add reproducible held-out information beyond matched pseudo-calendars.

### What ARIS4C013 must not claim from a positive offset-0 result
A same-birthday excess alone cannot establish:
- astrology;
- Bazi;
- a supernatural mechanism;
- causation by “birthday stress”;
- a universal human timing law.

It first establishes only an H2-type temporal association, conditional on surviving precision and null-model checks.
