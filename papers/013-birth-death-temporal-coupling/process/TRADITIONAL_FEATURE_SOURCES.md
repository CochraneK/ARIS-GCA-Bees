# Traditional-calendar convention sources · ARIS4C013

## High-authority calendar sources

### Hong Kong Observatory · Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches
The Observatory describes the 10 Heavenly Stems, 12 Earthly Branches and the 60-pair Gan-Zhi cycle.

Source:
https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/stemsandbranches.htm

### Hong Kong Observatory · 24 Solar Terms
The Observatory defines the 24 solar terms as 24 equal 15-degree divisions of solar ecliptic longitude.

Source:
https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/24solarterms.htm

### Hong Kong Observatory · Gan-Zhi year changeover
The Observatory explicitly distinguishes two conventions:

- its official calendar practice uses the first day of the first Chinese-calendar month as the Gan-Zhi year changeover;
- fortune tellers / fortune-telling enthusiasts often use Spring Commences / Li Chun.

This distinction is scientifically useful for ARIS4C013 because it allows the official cultural-calendar encoding and the Bazi-style encoding to remain separate rather than being silently mixed.

Source:
https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/education/astronomy-and-time/time-service/00506-what-year-is-it-today.html

## Practitioner-convention sources

Practitioner references consistently describe Four Pillars / Bazi as year, month, day and hour stem-branch pairs, with month boundaries tied to solar terms/Jie and hour calculations requiring birth time.

These sources are used only to document the convention being tested. They are not treated as scientific validation of the claims.

Examples reviewed during schema construction:
- DayPillar: https://daypillar.com/bazi-chart
- Clarify: https://clarifyhk.com/learn/bazi/calendar

## Scientific interpretation rule

A source can establish **what a traditional rule says** without establishing that the rule predicts health or mortality.

ARIS4C013 treats calendrical and practitioner sources as specification sources, while predictive validity must come entirely from prespecified held-out empirical tests.
