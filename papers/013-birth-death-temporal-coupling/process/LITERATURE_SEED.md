# Literature Seed · ARIS4C013

This is a seed map, not yet a systematic review.

## Birthday mortality
- **Ajdacic-Gross et al. (2012), Annals of Epidemiology.** “Death has a preference for birthdays—an analysis of death time series.” Swiss mortality 1969–2008; reported an overall 13.8% birthday excess. DOI: 10.1016/j.annepidem.2012.04.016.
- **Recent Swiss national analysis (1987–2022).** 1,702,865 adult deaths; reported 5,040 birthday deaths versus 4,665 expected, roughly an 8% excess after calendar adjustments.

## Month of birth and lifespan
- **Doblhammer & Vaupel (2001), PNAS.** “Lifespan depends on month of birth.” Austria, Denmark, and Australia; Northern/Southern Hemisphere patterns support environmental-seasonal mechanisms.
- **Doblhammer (1999), Demographic Research.** “Longevity and month of birth: Evidence from Austria and Denmark.”

## Chinese traditional belief / astrology and mortality
- **Phillips, Ruth & Wagner (1993), The Lancet.** “Psychology and survival.” 28,169 Chinese-American deaths and 412,632 matched white controls; reported shorter survival for selected disease × birth-year combinations treated as ill-fated. DOI: 10.1016/0140-6736(93)92124-C.
- **Smith (2006), Health Psychology.** “The five elements and Chinese-American mortality.” Critiqued exclusions and ambiguous mappings; independent California periods did not reproduce the original claim. DOI: 10.1037/0278-6133.25.1.124.

## Multiple-testing warning
- **Austin et al. (2006), Journal of Clinical Epidemiology.** “Testing multiple statistical hypotheses resulted in spurious associations: a study of astrological signs and health.” 10,674,945 Ontario residents and 223 diagnoses demonstrate how astrology-health signals can arise in discovery and fail validation. DOI: 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2006.01.012.

## Consequence for novelty
Novelty cannot be “birth date correlates with death.” The contribution is a unified competition among ordinary seasonal, anniversary, cultural, and traditional-calendar explanations with preregistration, matched pseudo-calendar nulls, and independent holdout replication.
