ARIS4C001 · research design · reconstructed 2026-09-18

Uncertainty Monitoring and Cross-Task Cognitive Covariation in Honey Bees

A falsifiable model-comparison project replacing the former single-precision/self-awareness simulation. The central question is now empirical: do learning ability and uncertainty-sensitive decisions share a common latent structure, form separable factors, or emerge from task-local associative processes?

What changed?

The old numerical claims were retired. Hand-picked beta distributions are no longer described as empirical fitting; covariance induced by a common simulated generator is no longer treated as independent validation; and the former precision optimum, negative GCA–metacognition correlation and disruption sign flip are not current results.

Confirmatory models

  1. One general factor across learning and uncertainty indicators.
  2. Two correlated factors: learning/GCA and uncertainty/control.
  3. Two independent factors.
  4. Trial-local associative opt-out model.
  5. Hybrid individual-factor + associative decision process.

A predictive-precision account is exploratory and must beat simpler models on held-out empirical data.

Current result

There is no new bee-effect estimate yet. The reconstruction concludes that published evidence is sufficient to motivate the model family but insufficient to estimate the coupling between learning/GCA and uncertainty monitoring because those phenotypes are not available together in the same individuals.

Reproducibility

The canonical research package lives at papers/001-gca-bees. Its model lock, evidence map, adversarial audit, synthetic model-recovery diagnostic and status gates are all public. Synthetic outputs are explicitly barred from being described as bee observations.
